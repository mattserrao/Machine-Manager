## Struck principles for HP showers
- Nozzles must create very laminar jets for efficient and gentle cleaning [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.18]
- Jets must be identical across the machine width for good CMD profile [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.18]
- Avoid nozzle plugging: water filtration and treatment; vacuum connection for purging the pipe; dedicated filter on each HP shower pipe; regular cleaning of the pipe through the FS ball valve with a pipe cleaning nozzle [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.19]
- Shower position may affect efficiency and operating issues [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.19]
- "Oscillation has to be state-of-the-art for efficiency and CMD uniformity." [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.20]
- Oscillation speed: V_osc = PM speed (m/min) x Jet diameter (mm) / (Cloth length (m) x 60) = mm/sec [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.20] (units as printed; the slide does not define "Cloth length" beyond that)
- "Reducing nozzle diameters allows significant water saving." [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.20]
- Use a portable stroboscope to see the jets; they look like faint streaks to the naked eye [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.11]

## Felt HP shower pressures (AstenJohnson, different basis: felt cleaning)
- Fine paper and coated board machines ran 150 psi (10.3 bar) to 250 psi (17.2 bar) with carefully maintained nozzles; unbleached packaging used over 300 psi (20.7 bar) with coarser cap deniers [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.19]
- Recommended: HP showers 3-4 inches from the felt surface; flaring needle jets damage the cap batt and show up as weight loss in returned felt analyses [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.19] [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.24]
