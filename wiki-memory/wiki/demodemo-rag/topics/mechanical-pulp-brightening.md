Source: [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-07]] pp.13-15. See also [[peroxide-ozone-enzyme-bleaching]], [[bleaching-sequences-and-stages]].

- Chemicals: sodium hydrosulfite (reductive) and peroxide (oxidative); modern two-stage peroxide can exceed brightness 80; no gain is fully stable to light and air [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.13]
- Hydrosulfite: 4-8% consistency, pH 5.5-6.5, 1-2 h at 60 C, application 1% or less on pulp [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.14]
- Losses rise with higher temperature, lower pH, poor mixing and entrained air; deaerate stock [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.14]
- Sodium tripolyphosphate (or silicate) chelates Fe/Cu; cedar and cypress mechanical pulps respond poorly, as does old or rotten/heartwood-rich wood [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.14]
- Peroxide: single stage adequate up to 15 points gain; medium consistency up to 10 points; above 10 points high consistency (presses to 25-40% before peroxide) [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.14]
- Gains above 15 points need peroxide residual recycle, so two stages (medium then high consistency) [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.14]
- Mixed peroxide/hydrosulfite sequence: effects largely additive; peroxide in downflow tower, SO2 injected at bottom, hydrosulfite in an upflow tower to exclude air [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.14]
