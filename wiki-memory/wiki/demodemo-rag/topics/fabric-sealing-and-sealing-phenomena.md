- Sealing means a material deforms under pressure so as to close off flow; it increases with fiber deformability, often with more refining of kraft fibers [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.37]
- "accepted wisdom among some papermakers that fabric sealing tends to be more of a problem if initial drainage is too rapid, for instance when the angle of impingement of the jet onto the forming fabric is too steep" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.8]
- Commonly recommended: "minimize the volume of early drainage" (Giles 1990); Miller: rapid drainage compresses fibers into the fabric, an inertial effect [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.38]
- Forming fabric permeability alone is a poor predictor of apparent permeability once a fiber layer interacts; flipping the fabric over changes dewatering significantly [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.38]
- Funnel model (Kufereth, Fig. 11): fibers bridging the wide part of each funnel give high permeability; fibers wedged in the throat cause sealing [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.38-39]
- Koponen et al.: flexible LWC and NSSC fibers gave 1.5% and 0.5% higher solids with gradually increasing pressure profiles vs constant pressure [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.37]
- Nordman: at 80 degrees Schopper-Riegler, air pressure gave no additional water passage [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.37]
- Dialcohol cellulose fibers: highly retained water after vacuum and very pronounced wire mark (Sjostrand 2019) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.39]
- Models: continuum models tend to overestimate sealing since density-resistivity relation is highly non-linear [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.37]
- Practical table link: uncontrolled vacuum on the early table can cause "sheet sealing" [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.3]; see [[doc-vacuum-dewatering-best-practices-part-1]]
