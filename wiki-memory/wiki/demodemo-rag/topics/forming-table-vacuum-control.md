- Table dewatering problems are noted on 90 percent of systems: no understanding of operation, no accurate vacuum measurement/control, no analysis of drainage studies, poor effect on sheet quality, table drive load and forming fabric life [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.2]
- Many fourdriniers have "uncontrolled and aggressive dewatering occurring at the initial one or two low vacuum elements"; signs are pulsing vacuum piping or whitewater from small separators [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.2-3]
- Confirm with a table drainage study; "one low vacuum element is unknowingly removing 10-30 percent of the total headbox flow" [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.3]
- When gauges, manometers, transmitters and valves fail, low-vac elements end up manually run "with most or all valves fully open", applying full header vacuum to every element [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.3]
- Effects: formation problems and sheet sealing that influences dewatering down the table [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.3]; see [[fabric-sealing-and-sealing-phenomena]]
- Max vacuum for a low-vac element = elevation of vacuum outlet above the seal pot whitewater overflow, "as little as 12" to as much as 40""; upper whitewater limit 2" below bottom of the outlet (Fig. 2, "It only requires a tape measure.") [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.3]
- Evidence of exceeding the limit: whitewater flowing into seal tanks on small separator drop legs at the back of the table; "significant whitewater passing through the vacuum outlet creates vacuum fluctuations and can cause CD and MD dewatering variations" [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.3]
- High-vacuum flatboxes near the wet/dry line are often too many or have too much slot area; "flatboxes do not need more than five or six slots" and slot widths decrease toward the couch [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.4]
- Reworked covers in silicon nitride ceramic plus fewer boxes: 10-20 percent power saved among couch, wire turning roll and other former drives [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.4]
- Best-practice list: accurate vacuum measurement and control for every element, know the early table setup, observe physical max limits, optimize, remove unneeded or high-slot-area flatboxes, consider low-drag ceramics [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.4]
- Compare Hubbe review: practice of "steadily increase the levels of vacuum" along the table; slots typically 13 to 25 mm wide [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.10-11]
