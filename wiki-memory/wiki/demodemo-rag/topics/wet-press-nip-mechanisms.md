- Single-felted nip: water moves in one direction into the felt; a dense layer forms next to that boundary and persists after drying [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.13]
- Stratification explanation (Campbell 1947): fibers nearest the exit lack hydraulic support, their cell walls and lumens are dewatered, giving a densified layer; Chang: stronger with low permeability, i.e. more refining [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.42-43]
- Freeze-dried specimens did not retain the stratified density structure (Szikla and Paulapuro 1989) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.43]
- Dewatering mainly on the entering side of the nip (Bergstrom 1959); some water returns on the expanding side [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.13]
- Terzaghi split of resisting pressure into structural and hydraulic (Wahlstrom) is questioned (Kataja 1995; McDonald): fibers hold much water and offer little mechanical resistance [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.13-14, p.27]
- Crushing: in-plane flow from high internal hydraulic pressure, causing damage when hydrodynamic forces exceed web integrity, e.g. excessive incoming moisture [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.14]
- Hornification: forcing water out of a refined kraft fiber cell wall can permanently lose swelling ability [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.14]
- Double-felted nip: symmetry and double the void capacity for removed water; may give wetter lightweight sheets via two-sided rewet [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.14]
- Extended nip: longer pressing area means lower force per unit area, more time for water to permeate, higher press impulse; "one of the most important contributions to paper machine speed and efficiency in recent decades" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.14]
- Shoe presses apply lower peak pressure but higher line load, giving greater press impulse; "Press impulse has been shown to be the dominant factor in press section water removal" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.27]
- Viscoelastic behavior (Kelvin spring-dashpot) used in models but the review judges the spring-and-dashpot concept flawed since it only predicts impulse dominance at vanishing dwell times [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.27]
- Some press rolls are drilled or grooved to avoid felt saturation and void pressure (Fig. 13) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.42]
- Prior solids matter: differences in solids after vacuum dewatering persist after wet pressing regardless of press level (Koponen 2012/2015) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.12]
