Source [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-03]]. Link to paper machine side: recovered steam for drying see [[dryer-section-drying]].

- TMP and CTMP are energy-intensive; "it is quite feasible to recover a large portion of the energy (up to 75%)" [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.18]
- Early recovery only made hot water from contaminated exhaust steam; modern systems make clean steam in a reboiler (falling-film boiling water outside tubes, condensing contaminated steam inside) [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.18]
- Recovered steam pressure is low (refiner pressure minus reboiler tube drop); for paper drying it may need a thermocompressor or mechanical compressor, which hurts economics [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.18]
- Refiner can run at much higher pressure than the presteaming vessel without hurting quality, since lignin does not pass the glass transition during brief retention; presteaming vessel isolated with plug screw or rotary valve [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.19]
- Energy balance: 64% of original electrical energy can be recovered as clean drying steam [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.19]
- Fig 5-37: electric 100%, TMP steam 4.2 bar abs 80%, clean steam 3.5 bar 74%, paper machine drying steam 3.3 bar 64% [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.19]
