## Supply and grades
- Secondary fiber use rose sharply in the 1980s-90s and is leveling off; legislation mandated minimum content for some grades, especially newsprint. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.9]
- Collecting beyond about 50 miles (80 km) from the mill is generally uneconomical. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.9]
- Five basic grades: mixed paper, ONP, OCC, pulp substitutes, high-grade deinked; pulp substitutes most valuable. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.9]
- Table 14-1 usage % (1977 / 1988 / 1999): mixed 19/11/18; ONP 16/16/19; OCC 43/49/48; pulp substitutes 15/15/6; high-grade deinked 7/9/9. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.9]
- About 75% of North American secondary fiber goes to multi-ply paperboard and corrugating medium. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.9]
- Table 14-2 OCC price US$/ton: 1994 140; 1995 210; 1996 60; 1997 120; 1998 70; 1999 95. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.10]
- Storage: segregate by grade and supplier, avoid outdoor storage, FIFO; fresher fiber deinks more easily. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.10]

## Recovery vs utilization (14.2)
- Recovery rate = wastepaper recovered / paper consumed; utilization rate = secondary fiber used / total fiber used. Different bases; do not mix. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.10]
- U.S. recovery 48% in 1999; Japan and Taiwan over 50%; Europe about 49%; Canada about 25%. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.10]
- Utilization: industrialized nations about 41%, Europe 43%, U.S. 40%, Japan 55%; Sweden and Canada about 15%. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.10]
- Practical overall maximum about 50%; some sources say fiber can be recycled about four times. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.10]

## Effect of recycling on properties
- Beaten bleached kraft (Fig 14-1): breaking length, burst and density fall; tear and scattering coefficient rise; attributed to hornification and hemicellulose loss. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.11]
- CTMP (Fig 14-2): the reverse: breaking length, burst, density rise; tear and scattering fall (fiber flattening, flexibility). [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.11]
- Hard-nip calendering can shorten fibers of all pulp types. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.11]

## Contaminants and screening (14.4)
- Process steps: screening, cleaning, washing, bleaching, dispersion, deinking; glues, hot melts, latexes lumped as "stickies". [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.13]
- Table 14-3 examples: hot melts foul equipment; pressure sensitives stick to fabrics and felts and cause web breaks; asphalt gives black spots; wet-strength resins slow the pulper; polystyrene foam causes pickouts. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.14]
- Screen slots: 0.008" early 1990s, then 0.006", then 0.004". [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.13]
- Micro-stickies agglomerate later, so some plants moved final fine screens to the end of the process or added a set ahead of the dryer. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.15]
- Dispersion (Fig 14-9): thicken to 35%, plug screw, shredder, preheater, disperser; pressurized max 1.3 bar (125°C); hot systems cleaner, cold adequate for filler plies. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.15]
- Vinyl acetates and polypropylene have defied removal and are best excluded. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.15]

## Machine impact (14.7)
- Treat each secondary pulp as a distinct furnish component with its own cleanliness, freeness and refining targets. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.21]
- Stickies and hot melts precipitate on fabrics and dryer surfaces; mills converting to secondary furnish need stricter fabric cleaning and/or boilout. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.21]
- Recycled sheets are more porous and absorbent: more ink needed, poorer resolution, higher rub-off; not for the most critical grades. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.21]
