- NSSC hardwoods and recycled corrugated have good compressive potential vs unbleached kraft but less favorable tensile; recycled medium fraction has high compressive strength [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.32]
- Worster: a relaxation of Rule 41 would allow more hardwood in linerboard; pine-only mills face harder barking, higher chipping energy, more chip-pile loss, lower bark heat value, possibly lower tall oil [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.32]
- Worster: pulping mixed softwood/hardwood "overcooks the hardwood portion"; separate cooks in the duplex bottom sheet: up to 20% green liquor hardwood (70% yield) with no major ring crush or tear effect, about 18% Mullen loss [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.32]
- Table IIa (0/5/10/15/20% green liquor pulp): Mullen 123/118/117/108/101; ring crush 67/66/65/65/65; total linerboard yield 54.2 to 57.4% on b.d. wood [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.33]
- Sproul (Herty Foundation, 31-inch Fourdrinier), up to 30% NSSC: CD ring crush constant; burst -19% to 99 psi on nominal 42 lb; tensile -13 to -18%; density 610 to 530 kg/m3 [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.33]
- Table III run 7167 (70% kraft / 30% NSSC): Mullen 99 vs 128 points (81%), CD tear 301 vs 406 g/sheet, CD ring crush 61 vs 60 lb, apparent density 0.53 vs 0.61 g/cc [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.34]
- Shick and Snow: ring crush insensitive to yield up to about 75%; sulfite-sulfide-carbonate at 70-75% yield could replace kraft at 50-60% [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.34]
- Koning (Philippine hardwood 50/50 with western kraft, 42 lb): low burst, needs starch sizing or 47 lb to reach 100+; top-load box compression about 9% below control [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.35-36]
- Bormett (Columbian hardwood 50/50): starch raised burst by as much as 30% to only about 90 psig; CD ring crush 4 to 34% below control; box strength below Philippine mixtures [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.36]
- Conclusion: about 20% or somewhat more hardwood feasible if lower burst is tolerated; optimization of pulping, refining, pressing needed; FPL press-drying suggests up to 100% hardwood can match low-yield softwood if pressed and dried to high density [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.36-37]
- Relative insensitivity of compressive strength to higher yield "provides further incentive" for hardwood use [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.37]
