- Principle: after forming board, foils and low-vacuum flatboxes, high-vacuum boxes follow the pattern of gradually more aggressive forces removing progressively smaller proportions of water [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51]
- Eames and Moore (1976) observed better dryness and energy use with a sharply graduated arrangement of vacuum levels [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51]
- Hansen (1985): begin flatbox dewatering at a pressure "low enough to minimize sealing phenomena", since the web has no mechanical integrity at the start; fibers facing the fabric must stay entwined in the mat or wire mark, fuzziness and sealing result [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51] [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- Time at each vacuum level = sum of slot widths at that level and machine speed; keep it short; reduce the number of boxes if there are too many [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- "a maximum of eight slots per box was recommended" (Miller 1998) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- Prolonged highest vacuum wastes three ways: pumping energy, forming fabric wear, and drive power to overcome fabric-to-cover friction [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- The plateau (little more water removed) is reached more quickly at higher vacuum (Gagnon and Neun 1996) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- Fig. 14 (after Baldwin 1997): three successive boxes V1 < V2 < V3 give higher solids than running only the lowest vacuum at equal energy; the example is "clearly oversimplified" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- Koponen et al. (2016): strong linear correlation between maximum achievable solids and the response to applied pressure difference [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51]
- Little has been published on low-vacuum dewatering optimisation [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51]
- No numeric vacuum levels (kPa or inHg) are given in these pages; numbers would come from the machine's own trials.
