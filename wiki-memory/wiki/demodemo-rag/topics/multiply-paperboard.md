## Definition and grades
- ISO dividing line between paper and board is 224 g/m2, roughly 0.010 inch (10 points) caliper [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.13]
- Multiply board is two or more web plies bonded into one sheet (rigid boxes, folding cartons); often specified by caliper not grammage [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.13]
- Table 19-1: linerboard (2+ plies, fourdrinier, 100% virgin), foodboard, folding boxboard (virgin top ply, secondary fibre other plies), chip board, base board, gypsum board [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.13]
- Cylinder machines can use cheap bulky waste (old newspapers) in filler plies and tolerate inks/contaminants [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.13]

## Formers
- Counterflow vat: about 80% of existing vats; plies up to 10 points; washes off part of deposit, blotchy formation; MD:CD tensile ratio about 5.0 for vat board vs below 2.0 for fourdrinier paper [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.14]
- Uniflow vat: better formation, less directionality, lower ply weight; plies up to about 2 points; on multi-vat machines uniflow for top and underliner, counterflow for filler and bottom liner [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.14]
- Conventional cylinder formers: speed up to about 110 m/min, width limited to about 4 m, poor CD grammage uniformity [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.14]
- Dry vat restricts stock to part of the vat to cut wash-off [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.14]
- Rotoformer (Sandy Hill, 1960s) suction former, plies usually above 10 points, up to 300 m/min with suction pickup instead of rubber couch [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.15]
- Beloit Hydraulic Former: pressure not vacuum, can be retrofitted onto an existing vat cylinder; simpler but slower than vacuum formers [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.15]
- Inverform (English, late 1950s): fourdrinier with secondary headboxes and top-wire formers; quality almost equal to fourdrinier sheet; only one North American mill installed it [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.16]
- Other on-top formers: Tampella, Arcu-Former (two wires), Ultraformer (Kobayashi; about 200 degrees of cylinder wrap, speed limited by centrifugal force) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.17]
- Multiple fourdriniers: practical limit about four plies, low to medium grammage, clean furnish [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.17]
- Multi-layer headbox: layered jet gives better plybond through mixing but poorer outer-surface coverage, so liner plies need better fibre or a separate former [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.18]

## Pressing and drying
- Heavy grades need gradual gentle pressing, keep web tensile stress low [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.18]
- Web consistency leaving each forming mold cannot go much above about 15% without hurting plybond [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.19]
- Pressure of about 100 pli can be applied at the soft double-felted suction drum nip without crushing the sheet [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.20]
- Dryer surface temperature should rise gradually; too-rapid steam generation traps steam pockets causing picking, blistering, ply separation; below about 20% moisture fast surface evaporation can cause case hardening [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.21]
- Top and bottom dryer tiers should have separate steam pressure control when the two sides differ [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.21]
- Differential drying between faces builds strain that releases as curl in converting; multi-furnish sheets most susceptible [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]

## Sheet finishing
- Water doctor finish: sheet enters at about 3% moisture and leaves at about 6%; pickup limited to about 1% per doctor [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.21]
- Machine glazing cylinder: one smooth glossy side, keeps more bulk than water doctor, limited to porous sheets of 450 g/m2 or less [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]

## Plybond
- Plybond is resistance to ply separation under tensile force perpendicular to the plies [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]
- If fines are removed from one or both surfaces, plybond falls by about half [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]
- Free water at the pickup nip: one ply below 9% consistency, the other can be 18-20% [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]
- Design maximum ply caliper 4 or 5 points; calendering is a major cause of plybond loss; on a machine with seven pressure formers, 26-point board is the maximum through a calender with 12-inch intermediate rolls [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]
- Rolling shear strength index (passes to failure through a loaded steel/rubber nip) is an alternative to the perpendicular rupture test [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]

## Stiffness
- Stiffness is the most important property for most multiply grades; each ply contributes in proportion to the square of its distance from the sheet centre times its tensile stiffness [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.23]
- Table 19-2, seven-ply board, % of stiffness: top liner 38.5, ply 2 4.3, ply 3 0.3, ply 4 0.5, ply 5 3.1, ply 6 8.3, back liner 45.0; liners together about 83.5% [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.23]

## Multi-ply linerboard and medium (FKBG report)
- Bergstrom, Beloit Strataflo/Converflo headbox at Obbola: 3-ply, virgin unbleached kraft outside, recycled/virgin mix in centre; 25% waste in centre ply gave burst comparable to 100% kraft, while single-layer mixing gave about 10% lower burst; stratified sheet had 25% higher bending stiffness [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.39]
- Bergstrom trial with 30% hardwood kraft in the middle ply and more refined softwood outside: 10% ring crush increase at lower furnish cost [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.39]
- Grossman, Voith Duoformer K: 3-ply OCC boards had somewhat higher burst and ring crush than single ply [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.39]
- MD/CD compressive ratios are much less affected by fibre orientation and drying strain than MD/CD tensile; literature insufficient to assess linerboard/medium laminates [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.40]
