## Pulpwood measurement
- Pulpwood is bought mainly on dry weight, estimated from volume or wet weight with a moisture/dry-density factor [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.11]
- Moisture 33% to 50% equals 0.5 to 1.0 kg water per kg dry wood [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.11]
- Cord = 8 ft x 4 ft x 4 ft stack, about 128 cu ft total volume, unreliable (air space, bark); cunit = 100 cu ft solid wood [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.11]
- Green weight (truck weighed loaded and empty) is the most popular method and needs a moisture estimate [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.12]
- Chip unit = 200 cu ft bulk, about one short ton of oven-dry wood [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.12]
- Conversions: 1 cord = 0.83 cunit = 1.04 unit; 1 cunit = 1.25 unit = 1.2 cord; 1 unit = 0.96 cord = 0.8 cunit [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.12]

## Woodroom flow and debarking
- Woodroom sequence: haul-up, slasher deck, barking, chipping, chip screening, rechipping of oversize, bins/silos [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.12]
- Northern mills deice and wash logs (hot-water showers in winter) to ease dry debarking and keep sand out of barkers [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.13]
- Drum barkers (most common): lose about 4 to 5% of wood and cause "brooming"; wet drums need bark dewatering and wastewater treatment [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.13]
- Ring (cambium shear) debarkers perform poorly on crooked or frozen logs [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.13]
- Rosser head debarkers suit frozen logs and strong-adhesion bark but wood losses are high [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.14]
- Hydraulic jet debarkers: jets over 1000 psig, wood loss usually under 2%, high capital/energy cost and hard-to-clean effluent [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.14]

## Chipping and screening
- Typical chipper: flywheel disc with radial blades, chips cut at about 20 mm [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.14]
- "Ideal: about 20 mm long, along the grain, 4 to 5 mm thick"; acceptable prime range 10 to 30 mm long, 3 to 6 mm thick [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.14]
- Keys: well-designed chipper, correct speed control, sharp knives [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.14]
- Modern disc or roll screens separate by thickness (industry standard); older screens separate only by length [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.15]
- Oversize goes to a rechipper or chip slicer (splits parallel to grain, less fibre damage than hammermill rechippers); fines usually burned with bark [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.15]

## Chip handling and storage
- Airveying covers about 300 to 400 m but uses much power and damages chips; belt conveyors cost more up front [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.16]
- Outside chip storage loses about 1% of wood substance per month (respiration, chemical reaction, microorganisms) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.17]
- Limit losses: concrete or asphalt base, screen before storage, avoid wind-blown fines and heat build-up, tall triangular pile at angle of repose [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.17]
- Build and reclaim first-in first-out; ring-shaped pile has two open faces (fed at one, reclaimed at the other) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.17]
- Extractives leach fastest in the first two months; sulfite chips should be stored, kraft mills recovering tall oil/turpentine should bypass storage with fresh chips [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.17]
- Surge bins meter and blend two or three species; chips flow poorly so bins use live-bottom devices, and hang-ups still occur after chips sit several days [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.18]

## Chip quality control
- Contaminants: oversize, pin chips (under 3 mm wide and thick), fines (pass 3 mm holes), bark, burnt wood, rotten wood, dirt [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.18]
- Oversize chips cause rejects in chemical pulping; pin chips, fines and rotten wood cut yield and strength; pin chips contain good fibre and are best pulped separately [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.19]
- Bark and burnt wood are dirt sources, worst in mechanical and sulfite mills; kraft is more tolerant as bark dissolves in alkaline liquor [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.19]
- Whole-tree chips: bark removal efficiencies of 70% achieved (air separation, flotation, compression debarking, Paprifer) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.19]
- Refiner mechanical pulping wants chips free of sand and grit; most systems include chip washing [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.19]
- See also [[mechanical-pulping]] for chip effects on refining [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.11]
