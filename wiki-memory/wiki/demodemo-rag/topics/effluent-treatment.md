## In-plant sources and abatement (26.2)
- Recycling and reuse of process water is the single most effective way to cut in-plant losses; limits are temperature and impurity buildup [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.8]
- Paper machine white water: large volumes, often reusable; minimum rule is that all unused white water goes to a saveall (filter or flotation) to recover solids; clarified water can be used in showers and seals [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.9]
- Digester and evaporator condensates are concentrated BOD sources, sent to a steam stripper (Fig. 26-3); noncondensables burned in lime kiln or power boiler [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.9]
- Bleach plant: biggest in-plant BOD and colour reduction comes from oxygen delignification; also optimal counter-current washing [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.9]
- Regulatory history: 1970 National Environmental Policy Act and EPA; 1972 Federal Water Pollution Control Act Amendments, 1977 target best practicable technology (BPT) [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.9]. Doc p.10 is blank in the notes.

## Primary treatment
- Primary treatment mainly removes suspended solids; main methods are gravity sedimentation (most common) and dissolved-air flotation [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.11]
- Circular clarifier: underflow pumped at about 1.5 to 6% solids; surface loading typically 800 to 1000 gal/day/ft2; minimum depth 11 ft for 30-65 ft diameter (15 ft over 200 ft); floor slope 1 to 12 [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.12]
- Four zones: inlet, settling, outlet, sludge; design needs settling tests on the actual wastewater [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.12]
- Flotation can remove up to 98% of suspended solids with alum-type flocculant, but compressor power and chemicals are costly; in pulp and paper mostly used to clarify white water [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.13]

## Secondary (biological) treatment
- Aerobic requirements: biodegradable non-toxic waste, DO present, nutrients (N, P) as needed, pH 6.5 to 8.5, temperature preferably 25-35 C (tolerate to 40 C) [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.13]
- Storage oxidation lagoon: 3-4 ft deep, 85-90% BOD removal needs about 30-day detention and about 20 acres per MGD; rarely used today [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.14]
- Aeration stabilization basin (ASB): DO at least 0.5 ppm, detention 5-10 days (up to 15), BOD removal about 90%, cost about half of activated sludge; below 18 C removal can fall to about 50% [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.15]
- Activated sludge: 3-8 h detention, DO 1-2 ppm (pure oxygen 6-8 ppm with 1.5-2.0 h), up to 95% BOD reduction; F/M ratio typically 0.2 to 0.4 [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.15] [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.16]
- Biological filters and rotary discs grow films on media [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.16] [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.17]
- Anaerobic UASB removes about 80% of BOD and yields methane-rich biogas [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.17]

## Colour, zero effluent, sludge
- Colour comes from tannin and lignin derivatives; lime coagulation is the most common removal method (Fig. 26-16); others are alum/polymer, membranes, activated carbon, rapid infiltration [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.17] [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.18]
- Unbleached and recycled paperboard mills have eliminated effluent; no bleached kraft mill had yet done so (as of the book) [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.19]
- Sludge dewatering: Canadian survey average cake consistency 31% for screw presses and 27% for belt presses; landfilling is usually final disposal, leachate is the main concern [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.20]
