## Formulation
- "Pigments make up 70-85% by weight of the finished coating"; binder 15-30%; additives under 1% each; coating colour total solids about 50% to over 70% [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.2]
- China clay is the most common pigment; alkaline papermaking lets mills use calcium carbonate; TiO2 only for specialty grades [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.2, p.3]
- Binders: starches, proteins (casein, soya, animal glue - can spoil), synthetics (SB, acrylic, vinyl acetate latex) increasingly replacing them; keep binder at lowest level that meets end use, excess fills voids and reduces light scattering [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.3]
- Additive classes (Table 18-3): insolubilizers, plasticizers, rheology control, dispersants, preservatives, defoamers, dyes [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.3]

## Kitchen and rheology
- Coating is 25-35% of sheet weight; a 600-1100 tonnes/day coated machine needs about 200-400 tonnes/day of colour [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.4]
- Batch systems suit infrequent transitions and let quality be checked before the coater; continuous systems make colour just in time and dump little on grade change [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.4]
- Strainers at almost every stage; temperature usually controlled by a heat exchanger on the line to the coater head [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.4]
- Starch solutions and pigment suspensions are non-Newtonian (viscosity depends on strain rate) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.4]

## Coaters
- On-machine: less handling, fewer operators, early raw-stock problems; off-machine: flexibility, easier grade changes, generally better coating quality (Table 18-4) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.5]
- Air-knife: cross-machine air jet blows off excess; rod coater suits heavier grades only [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.5]
- Blade coaters (1950s): pond, trailing blade, inverted blade, vacuum blade types; coat weight set by blade angle and pressure; bent blades allow higher coat weights and scratch less [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.6]
- Two-sided: opposed blade coater was hard to control; later designs use a backing roll on one side and a blade on the other [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.6]
- Cast coating gives exceptional gloss; wet coated paper pressed onto a glazed chrome cylinder; base sheet must be fairly porous [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.6]
- Short-dwell problem: the gap between applying and metering lets water and binder migrate into the sheet, so coating composition becomes inconsistent [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.7]
- Short-dwell device (1980s, Consolidated Paper): turbulence in the pond, pressure held by supply pump and set by the exit weir [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.8]
- OptiSpray (2001, Metso Paper): non-contact high-pressure atomised spray of colour or size to both sides at once, less web pressure, less dependence on base sheet quality [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.8]

## Drying coated paper and sheet properties
- Drying options: steam cylinders only where they do not disturb the coating; hot air impingement and infra-red most common alternatives; high-velocity hoods over cylinders for single-sided coatings; tunnel drying; gas-fired infra-red emitters give heat only so air must remove moisture [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.8]
- Air-float dryers follow each on-machine blade coating stage (Fig 18-21); off-machine layout in Fig 18-22 [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.9]
- Five factors controlling coated sheet properties: raw stock surface, coating composition, coating method, drying method, extent of supercalendering [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.8]
- Raw stock roughness controls coat thickness uniformity; absorptivity controls composition of the layer (capillary forces pull water-soluble components into the sheet) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.8]
- Coating structure by method: air knife follows base sheet contours; blade fills valleys but thickness less uniform; film and rod intermediate [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.9]
- Fast drying or slow absorption changes final structure, worse as coating speed rises [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.9]
- Drying shrinks the coating by about 45 to 70 %, bringing base sheet roughness back, worst at low coat weights [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.10]
- Kaolin platelets start randomly oriented; supercalendering aligns them for gloss, see [[calendering]] [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.10]
