## Early drainage and sealing
- Avoid excessive early dewatering: too fast mat formation on the forming fabric gives high flow resistance, "fabric sealing" (Giles 1990) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.47]
- Flocculating stock with polymer greatly promotes early dewatering but can hurt dryness at the couch because a flocky sheet responds poorly to vacuum [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.47]
- Pressure forming = steep jet impingement; velocity forming = jet nearly parallel to the fabric, fibers lie down flat and are less likely to be pushed into the fabric voids [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.47]
- Breast roll discharge (jet lands so close to the breast roll that it acts like a big table roll) "is generally to be avoided"; harsh impingement also adds wire mark and lowers fine-particle retention [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.48]
- A finer weave on the sheet side can resist sealing; fabric air permeability is a poor predictor of dewatering rate (except lightest tissue) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.48] [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.53]
- Consensus fabric: coarse structure on the machine side (wear, dimensional stability), finer, narrower filaments on the sheet side matched to furnish and grade smoothness [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.53]
- Forming board under the fabric at jet impingement blocks early drainage; Eames (1993) hydrofoil has a designed pressure pulse that pushes water back into the sheet [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.48]

## Thickening / refluidizing (hydrofoils)
- Dewatering is a filtration process, so each unit operation stratifies the sheet; overly aggressive foils or blades reduce strength (roll-blade twin-wire forming shown) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.48]
- Step blade (Cadieux 1983): small initial land touching the fabric, rest at a lower plane; gives a distinct pressure signature [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.49]
- Microturbulence ("spouting") can be tuned with a stroboscope on the early table; moderate activity applied early gave the best results (Ahonen 1992) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.49]
- Excessive spouting (droplets thrown off the peaks) gives a dusty sheet and strongly hurts fine-particle retention; the retention aid then gets increased and the sheet turns flockier [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.49]
- Closer foil spacing gives finer-scale microturbulence; pulsation frequency should exceed the rate of mat formation (Giles 1990) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Fines wash out of the layer next to the fabric on commercial Fourdriniers; that lowers resistance for later foils; recirculated with white water to the fan pump [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Formation uniformity: a flocky sheet wastes vacuum energy (air through thin spots, water left in floc areas); uniform formation also gives higher solids after vacuum dewatering [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51]

## Rewetting
- Remedies: separate the web immediately after the nip (no effect on separation rewet), pickup felt after a flatbox (Leinonen 2001), strong vacuum on the felt side of the outgoing nip (Tamfelt system), finer filaments in the press felt surface [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.47] [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.53]
- Speculation: moderate rewetting between successive vacuum and press events relaxes the densified surface layer and may help the next dewatering step; "Additional study would be required" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.54]
- Lucas-Washburn: rewetting into a densified (pressed) layer is slower, which limits rewetting [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.56]

## Heat
- Heat water before dewatering because viscosity falls; process water about 30 to 50 C; hotter makes fog over the Fourdrinier table and risks boiling the stock under the highest vacuum [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.56]
- Steam boxes are more valuable later (less water to heat): common just before wet-press nips, sometimes before vacuum flatboxes; about 1% dryness per 6 C but more sheet crushing risk [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.56] (compare [[press-section-design]] hot pressing: 1 point per 10 C, Smook)

## Fiber and refining
- Refining generally slows dewatering, but an intermediate refining level can give the highest dryness under vacuum (Raisanen 1995b) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.57]
- Stiff fibers give a bulky mat with open water paths; dried-and-reslurried kraft drains faster if not re-refined [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.57]
- Fines hurt gravity drainage (freeness) but also densify the sheet; secondary fines (from refining) strengthen kraft paper more than primary fines; moderate fines can help vacuum dewatering [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.57] (see [[freeness-csf]], [[refining-mechanisms-and-variables]])
- Cellulase/xylanase after refining can raise dewatering rate with strength kept; too much dose, time or temperature degrades fibers [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.57] [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.58]
- Hydrophobized fibers: as little as 5% added to hardwood or softwood kraft markedly raised CSF and lowered water retention value (Hakovirta 2014) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.58]

## Closing lessons
- Not every mechanism matters on every machine; fiber type, refining, basis weight and equipment differ [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.61]
- "At the start of the process, the wet web has almost zero mechanical strength, but water will flow from it quite easily. Applying severe forces of pressing at that point would make no sense" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.62]
- Bottleneck named: lack of realistic material laws for wet fiber networks and two-phase (water and air) flow models [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.61]
