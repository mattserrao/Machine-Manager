## Condensate behaviour and removal
- Condensate regimes as speed rises: slow-speed puddle, climbing puddle, cascading, rimming; "Rimming ... usually starts between about 1200 and 1400 ft/min." [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.10]
- Differential pressure to push condensate from shell to centreline journal is large at speed (example about 20 psi at 3500 ft/min) [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.10]
- Blow-through steam entrains condensate and removes non-condensibles; "typically runs at 15-20% of the condensate mass flow" [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.10]
- Higher differential pressure increases blow-through, which can erode piping and reduce flexibility, so it is controlled just above the minimum needed [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.10]
- Wide machines commonly use two siphons, one at each end [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.10]
- Steam enters at the backside and condensate leaves at the front (tending side) on the newer arrangement (Fig 17-10) [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.10]

## Siphon sizing and type
- Steam specific volume at the wet end is about three times that at the dry end; correct sizing gives about 20% blow-through [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.11]
- Slow machines: stationary siphon angled into the puddle; intermediate: generally rotating; high speed: either [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.11]
- Rotating siphon: differential pressure rises sharply with speed, example about 1.4 bar at 1000 m/min, "a major reliability problem for rotating siphons"; advantage is small tip-to-shell clearance [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.11]
- Stationary siphon: bends 90 degrees and ends within a couple of millimetres of the shell with a Teflon shoe pointing upstream; kinetic energy of rimming condensate removes it, pressure drop still needed at start-up [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.11]
- "North American industry has largely switched to stationary siphons since 1992." [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.11]
- Reducing siphon clearance matters more as speed rises to keep the condensate film thin [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.11]

## Dryer bars and steam systems
- Dryer bars (spoiler bars) fixed axially inside the shell turn the film's peripheral motion into a sloshing wave, lowering thermal resistance; segments at cross-machine positions correct moisture profile; Fig 17-13 shows a Beloit installation with 25 bars [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.12]
- Cascade system: blow-through steam reused in lower-pressure groups; drawback is sections depend on each other and only the highest-pressure section is independent [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.12]
- Thermo-compressor system: low-pressure blow-through boosted by high-pressure motive steam, reused usually in the same section; independent pressure control per section [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.12]
