## Settings (Heimbach)
- Pressure "15 to 40 bar"; nozzle-to-sheet distance "approx. 70-100 mm"; distance is only of secondary importance if jet is laminar, at right angle and pressure [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.2]
- Jet should be laminar, thin, smooth, no air; a turbulent jet breaks into droplets, gives a dirty cut and severe fibre misting [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.2]
- A turbulent jet can push MD and CD monofilaments out of alignment, causing wear at crossings, fibrillation and reduced MD tensiles in the forming fabric [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.2]
- Check jet and cut running with a hand stroboscope at about 50-60 Hz held flat above the fabric [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.2]
- Filtration: jet diameters 0.4 to 0.5 mm need a filter element of maximum 200 um mesh; pre-filtration 50 um or less before the pump [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.3]
- Water 50 to 60 C, warmer than ambient to avoid condensation build-up on nozzles [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.3]
- Smaller jet diameter gives better cut; with increasing wood content use smaller diameter nozzles and higher pressure [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.3]
- Reducing distance reduces fibre misting but increases stock build-up on the nozzle [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.4]

## Nozzle type and speed
- Up to about 500 m/min single-jet nozzles are generally adequate; at higher speeds with suction pick-up do not use two single-jet nozzles in series (mist from the first builds up on the second); use twin-jet or triple-jet [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.4]
- Jets slightly offset in MD so the next jet is within the first cut but marginally nearer the fabric edge [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.5]

## Angles
- MD tilt 15 to 40 deg from vertical reduces bounce back and mist; jet vector velocity should be close to fabric speed [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.5]
- Example: 0.4 mm nozzle, 20 bar, jet about 2600 m/min, machine 1200 m/min gives about 28 deg [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.5]
- CD tilt about 3 to 12 deg toward the trim depending on grade, basis weight, speed; "There is no precise rule or 'scientific formula'" [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.6]

## Placement and hardware
- Fourdrinier: before the suction couch; gap formers at high speed: after couch roll or HiVac, else suction further couches the trimmed sheet and separates badly at pick-up [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.6]
- Usually installed after a suction box; graphic gap formers mostly after the HiVac; packaging gap formers and Fourdriniers between last suction box and couch roll; if on a suction box let the jet hit a suction slot [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.6]
- Nozzles must be secure, vibration free, adjustable in CD and MD angle, and the unit easily removable for fabric changes [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.7]
- Poor edge trims can cause breaks in press, dryers, size press or coater; break cameras synchronised in real time can prove it [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.1]
- Edge Breaks causes list (trim squirt plugged, recouching, trim too small ...) is in [[forming-section-troubleshooting]]

## Struck / PaperCon 2011 (ruby nozzles; different pressure basis, see [[doc-ruby-nozzles-trim-squirts-hp-showers]])
- "Edge trimming and sheet break frequency are directly related."; double jet nozzles required at higher machine speeds [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.2]
- Fast machines need "400 to 500 psi (around 30 bar)" for a small jet diameter with high kinetic energy; insufficient pressure forces big nozzles and a poor cut [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.10]
- Tail cutter overhead under the pickup felt: single jet .025" to .030", "200 psi or less"; on the forming fabric use the same nozzle, pressure and geometry as the drive-side squirt [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.9]
- Modern nozzles seal with O-rings and metric cylinder thread (no Teflon tape); triple jet nozzles claimed lifetime beyond conventional [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.6] [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.7]
- Fiber build-up on trim nozzles and poor cut cause pickup problems and fiber bridges at trim separation after the 1st press [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.12] [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.13]
- AstenJohnson: near 15% solids or less squirts struggle to cut a clean edge; jet angled slightly outward and a few degrees downstream; see [[sheet-control-press-section]] [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.6]
- CONFLICT-ish: Heimbach 15-40 bar [DOC dcdb0d87-3ad5-4f05-8bb0-5454dea7c99c p.2] vs Struck 400-500 psi (about 28-34 bar) for fast machines [DOC 9e4de3b6-86e4-4f21-98a8-d0cd74271432 p.10]; overlapping range, same unit family.
