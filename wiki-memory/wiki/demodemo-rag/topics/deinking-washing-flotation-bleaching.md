## Chemistry
- Ink particle size matters: washing wants under about 10 um; "Flotation alone needs ink particles larger than about 50 µm". [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.16]
- Surfactants: detergents (remove ink), dispersants (keep it suspended), foaming agents; caustic soda, sodium silicate, borax boost action. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.16]

## Washing
- Before 1990 washing was the main method in North America: low capital, small space, high ash and fines removal, but low yield. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.16]
- Fig 14-10 wash plant: final thickener output about 4-5% consistency, rejects about 2.0%, second washing stage vertical screen about 1.0%. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.16]
- Very small ink (under about 10 um): removal is proportional to water used; the fiber mat filters and lowers actual removal. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.17]
- Ideal inlet consistency 1.0-1.5%; common practice about 3% inlet in countercurrent washing. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.17]
- Double-nip thickener can thicken to 8-12% in one stage. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.17]
- Table 14-4 (inlet to discharge %, one-stage / three-stage ink removal, dilution water gal/ton): sidehill screen 0.8-3.0, 74.0 / 98.2%, 26,740; gravity decker 0.9-6.0, 85.5 / 99.7%, 23,740; inclined screw 3.0-10.0, 72.2 / 97.8%, 6,950; horizontal press 4.0-28.0, 89.2 / 99.9%, 5,150. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.17]

## Flotation
- Grey stock aerated at 0.8-1.2% consistency in a series of cells; typically 6-10 cells in series, froth cleaned in a secondary stage of usually two cells. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.17]
- Clay needed: minimum 8-10%, preferred 12-14%; about 25-30% of the clay leaves in flotation rejects; coated waste is blended in to reach the level. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.19]
- Cells shown: Voith, Spidercel (Comer), Shinhama; older plants upgrade to high-efficiency cells and finer air nozzles. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.19]

## Washing vs flotation
- Flotation favored in Europe and Japan: more selective, much higher yield, needs little or no washing after, simpler effluent treatment. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.19]
- Washing removes fines and fillers with ink, upgrading pulp quality. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.19]
- Combined: washing wants hydrophilic fine ink, flotation hydrophobic flocs of about 50 um; "displector" surfactants (dispersant plus collector) bridge this; some designers float first, then wash. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.20]

## Bleaching (14.6)
- Peroxide in the pulper prevents alkaline darkening; a dedicated stage after contaminant removal gives larger gains. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.20]
- Oxygen: effective, cheap, removes colour tint, used with an EOP stage. Peroxide: versatile, relatively expensive, not strong enough alone for colour strip or lignin. Ozone: strong, chlorine-free, can destroy optical brighteners. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.20]
- Reductive agents: sodium hydrosulfite gives modest gains, cheap, low consistency; FAS gives higher gains, more expensive. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.21]
