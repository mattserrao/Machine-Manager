## Multiple effect
- Water removal is done in a series of bodies at successively lower pressures; vapor from each body heats the next [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.1]
- Figure 10-4: up to about 5.5 lb water per lb steam for seven effects; roughly 0.8 per effect (0.8 at 1 effect, 4.8 at 6) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.1]
- Weak liquor is fed at the low-pressure end (effects 6 and 5); moving toward effect 1 pressure, boiling temperature and solids rise [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.1]
- Boiling point rise grows with solids and limits the practical number of effects [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.2]

## Product solids
- "Strong black liquor leaves at about 50-60% solids"; firing needs "65-70% solids or higher" via direct-contact evaporator or concentrator [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.2]
- Salts have limited solubility, so concentrators foul and scale; large circulation rates reduce deposits; single-stage units cleaned often, sometimes with a spare [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.4]

## Types
- Rising-film is the most common existing type; falling-film is growing (more energy-efficient, better turndown) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.2]
- Fouling: switching arrangements let low-solids liquor wash deposits, extending run time before a water boilout [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.2]
- Table 10-1 (six-effect example): heating surface 4,400 ft2 for IA/IB and 8,800 ft2 for II-VI; vacuum reaches 22.0 in Hg at VI; liquor 252 F (IA) to 128 F (VI) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.2]
- Alternatives: blow-heat evaporators, mechanical vapor recompression (compressor lifts vapor temperature), thermal recompression (steam-jet) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.3]
- Direct-contact evaporator was used in nearly all recovery boilers until about 1968; sits after the economizer and strips reduced sulfur [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.3]
- Gas-side replacements: cascade evaporator (often C-E boilers) and cyclone evaporator (often B&W) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.4]
- Most common concentrator: falling-film with recirculation (Fig 10-12) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.4]
- Blow heat recovery can cut liquor evaporation load by 25-35% (reference 2) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.27]
