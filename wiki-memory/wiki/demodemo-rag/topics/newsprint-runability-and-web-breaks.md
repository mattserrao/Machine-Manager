## Newsprint basics
- Standard North American newsprint 48.8 g/m2 (30 lb per 3000 sq ft), brightness 58-60; many European publishers use 45 g/m2, some 40 g/m2 [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.25]
- Furnish mainly mechanical pulp, often with deinked recycled newsprint and some lightly refined chemical pulp; violet-blue dye offsets yellow; stone groundwood alone may need up to 30% chemical pulp [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.25]
- Publishers seek runability, printability and appearance (Table 20-2) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.25]

## Moisture and furnish economics
- Sold at "as is" grammage: running 9-10% moisture instead of 6-7% adds weight that is sold [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.25]
- Semi-bleached kraft costs about twice as much as typical mechanical pulp; cutting kraft from 11% to 10% of furnish could add about half a million dollars per year on a modern high-speed machine [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.25]
- A large high-speed machine can save close to $1 million a year in furnish cost running higher moisture because runability improves [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- Figure 20-1: higher moisture (5.0 to 9.0% axis) gives fewer breaks per roll, data from four ANPA pressroom systems [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- Older machines sometimes over-dry to build a uniform reel, compensating for non-uniform pressing and drying [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]

## Runability and break statistics
- Runability is the combined effect of sheet and roll properties deciding whether the press runs at the needed rate; poor runability shows as breaks, slack tension, wrinkles, poor register [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- Relationship to conventional strength parameters is poorly defined; some favour CD tear, others tensile [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- Table 20-3, three-year record, suppliers A-H, rolls per break: A 54 (8,001 rolls, 147 breaks), B 46, C 37, D 34, E 29, F 21, G 18, H 16 (10,522 rolls, 656 breaks) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- Rolls per break is not comparable across mills: a 50-inch roll holds about 50% more paper than a 42-inch roll and break definitions differ [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- Newsprint breaks are rare, roughly once per 300 miles of web [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- Table 20-4 survey, 1,091 breaks, mills A-F: paper defects 49%, roll defects 51%; largest single cause glue on roll end (212, 117 at mill D); loose paper in roll 96, faulty core 91, out-of-round roll 86, slime hole 81 [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]
- The printed fibre-cut total reads "933" but its six mill values add to 393; the 1,091 total and 49/51 split only work with 393 [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.26]

## Why webs break
- A break needs a flaw, low flaw-carrying ability and high tension together (Figure 20-2); flaws include slivers, slugs, slime, faulty splices, loose paper, glue on roll ends, cuts, slitter nicks [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.27]
- Flaw-carrying ability: % chemical pulp, % moisture, specific energy, burst, tear, formation, tensile, stretch, basis weight variation [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.27]
- Press tension factors: winding evenness and tension, transit damage, core condition, press accelerations, roll changes, flying pasters [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.27]
- Break only when a flaw coincides with a tension peak; Sweden study: paper strength always at least five times the web tension at break [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.27]
- Figure 20-4 (after Roisum): break splits into low strength (low average, high variance, fracture toughness or flaws) and high load (high set-point tension, high variance across width, span or time) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.27]

## Printability
- Printability depends on smoothness, absorbency, moisture, formation, opacity, brightness; cannot be captured by one number [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.27]
- Higher ink for print density also raises show-through; set-off (wet ink to next unit/page) and rub-off worsen with more ink; optimum pore size distribution gives moderate ink application [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.28]
