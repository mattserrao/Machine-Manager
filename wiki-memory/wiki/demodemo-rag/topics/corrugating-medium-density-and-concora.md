- Flat crush depends on MD properties of medium; combined board ECT depends mainly on CD compressive properties of the medium, so fiber orientation and drying restraint shift the balance [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.47]
- Initial part of the flat-crush load-deflection curve is critical to whether crushing in finishing degrades board; little information relating it to medium properties [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.47]
- Brecht and Bachmayer: CMT = (K E t^2 W)^k with k = 0.65; assuming E proportional to rho^2 it reduces to CMT = (K W^3)^k, implying density has no effect [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.48]
- Report's own handsheet check (Fig. 21; slope 0.65 vs 0.38 envelope): relation "did not apply well", mostly reflects basis weight; speculated to overemphasize caliper [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.48-49]
- Fig. 22: Concora and ring compression increase with density at every basis weight (19, 26, 33, 40 lb/1000 ft2), effect larger at higher basis weight; Fig. 23: Concora rises with compressive strength [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.48-51]
- Conclusion: for a given furnish, increasing density generally raises both Concora and edgewise compressive strength of medium [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.51]
- Concora reflects MD compressive strength, combined board strength reflects CD [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.51-52]
- "the conventional ring test on medium usually results in a buckling failure well below the intrinsic compressive strength" and may mislead because of caliper effects [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.52]
