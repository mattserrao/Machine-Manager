- "Yes, paper machines often have excess vacuum capacity"; typical opportunity to lower operating power "by 10-20 percent" [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.8]
- Inbleed valves that never close or have high setpoints are a lever; so are eliminating press suction rolls (older containerboard machines and pulp dryers), and removing unnecessary flatboxes and uhle boxes [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.8-9]
- Largest case: 360" printing paper machine removed 1.2 megawatts, a 40 percent horsepower reduction [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.9]
- Best practices: investigate operation with open inbleed valves; detailed vacuum-dewatering analysis and audit; remove unneeded boxes; eliminate suction rolls with roll cover shops and fabric suppliers; "Monitor vacuum pump motor loads and investigate changes, up and down" [DOC 56ec08f5-d829-4786-b810-b1a7de9c7f74 p.9]
- Scale: vacuum boxes "can consume one-fifth of the electricity used on a typical paper machine" (Hubbe review citing Håkansson 2010; Nilsson 2014b) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.2]
