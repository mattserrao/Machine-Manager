- Rewetting is real after high-vacuum flatboxes, at the couch, and in the outgoing nip; but its cause and size are disputed and there is no independent measure of web water inside the nip [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.43]
- Flatbox rewetting is attributed to water taken from the forming fabric, which holds "about 100 to 600 g/m2 of water"; web and fabric stay together several more seconds [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.43-44]
- Nip: Jaavidaan et al. estimated 30% of the water pressed from a sheet is taken up again [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.44]
- McDonald and Kerekes (2018) model: a "flow" rewet term (in-nip plus post-nip, proportional to square root of contact time) and a "separation" rewet term proportional to diameter of the surface batt fibers of the felt [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.44]
- Film splitting: water at the paper-felt interface divides about evenly on separation; basis weight did not significantly change the water taken up, so low basis weight sheets gain proportionately more [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.46]
- Wahlstrom: time does not significantly affect rewet after a nip; McDonald and Kerekes: rewet water is a linear combination of squeezed-out water and film-split water [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.46]
- Elastic re-expansion: spring-back to about half of incoming thickness after nip midpoint (Burton and Sprague 1987); high-yield and mechanical pulps expand more [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.44]
- Counter-argument (Paulapuro): tight capillaries and low restorative forces make elastic expansion an unlikely route; densified layer slows rewet (I'Anson and Ashworth) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.44-45]
- Felts are highly permeable and "become filled by air and water within about 3 ms" so they do not support suction to pull water away [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.45]
- Flatbox rewetting was large only when vacuum was strong enough to compress the sheet (Sjostrand 2015) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.45]
- Air content after pressing: CTMP about 40% air by volume vs 1 to 4% for refined bleached kraft [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.45]
- Membrane evidence (MacGregor 2002): 1 to 2 um colloidal membrane on the felt-side surface, consistent with water drawn back into the web [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.46]
- Double-felted nips can give a wetter web for lightweight paper via rewetting on both sides [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.14]
