## Overview
- Perhaps 10% of paper cost is chemicals, about $10 billion/yr on roughly $100 billion of North American shipments; clay is the highest tonnage additive, over half in coatings. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.22]
- Table 15-1 classes: acids and bases, alum, sizes, dry-strength adhesives, wet-strength resins, fillers, colorants, retention aids, flocculants, defoamers, drainage aids, brighteners, pitch control, slimicides, specialties. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.22]

## Sizing
- Wet-end sizing leaves the sheet porous; a vapor barrier needs a surface coating. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.25]
- Rosin (saponified, from tall oil) is precipitated onto fibers by alum as an oriented monolayer of aluminum resinate; rosin and wax are nonreactive sizes. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.25]
- Synthetic reactive sizes (1950s on) bond to cellulose hydroxyls as esters and allow alum-free sizing. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.25]
- AKD: first and still most widely used reactive size; best at pH 7-9, often with calcium carbonate; reacts slowly so full sizing cannot be measured on the machine. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.26]
- ASA: faster, best around pH 6-8; emulsions hydrolyze in water so are made at the mill just before use; needs good first-pass retention to avoid deposits. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.26]

## Strength
- Dry strength: starches (cooked before use), gums, cellulose derivatives, synthetic polymers; raise burst and tensile, reduce fuzz and lint. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.26]
- Wet strength: paper retaining more than 15% of dry tensile when wet, some grades up to about 50%; main agents urea-formaldehyde, melamine-formaldehyde, polyamide resins, cured in the dryers; anionic resins need alum only after rosin has precipitated. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.26]
- Table 15-5 (Britt 1963): ordinary paper tensile 13.7 dry, 12.1 in petroleum, 3.6 ethanol, 0.4 in water. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.27]

## Fillers
- Most filled papers contain 10-15% filler by weight; heavily loaded grades above 30%; limits are strength, bulk and sizing. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.27]
- Clay most popular; calcium carbonate only in neutral/alkaline systems, brighter and a better opacifier; talc soft feel and captures pitch; TiO2 brightest, used about 2-3%. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.27]

## Dyes and brighteners
- Dye types: acid (fixed with rosin and alum), basic, direct (adsorb readily on cellulose). Basic dyes are the main class for paper: low cost, high strength, poor light/acid/alkali/chlorine fastness. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.28]
- Optical brighteners (mostly stilbene derivatives) absorb UV and emit blue light; best on high-whiteness pulps, little effect on mechanical or unbleached pulps. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.29]
