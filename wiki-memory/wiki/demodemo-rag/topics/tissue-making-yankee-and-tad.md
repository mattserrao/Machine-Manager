- Tissue lowest weights about 5 g/m2; wet sheet is supported through forming, pressing and drying and no tension is applied until dry [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.3]
- Traditional layout: fourdrinier forming plus Yankee dryer; lick-up felt (relatively non-permeable, run moist) sometimes replaces suction pickup for lighter tissues [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.3]
- Yankee is a large steam cylinder with a high-velocity air impingement hood (Figure 20-15) [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.3]
- Creping happens when the sheet adheres to the Yankee and meets the square edge of the creping doctor; creping effect increases with higher contact angle [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.3]
- Yankee coating (adhesion/release) governs creped sheet quality; a controlled coating reduces blade-change frequency, built through wet end additives or sprayed on the dryer [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.5]
- Modern high-speed formers are gap roll formers in "C" or "S" configurations; Crescent former (Kimberly-Clark, 1960s) forms between felt and fabric [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.6]
- With secondary fibre, stickies can build on the inner (conveying) or outer (backing) wire; on the conveying wire they cause fewer pin holes; stratified headboxes place secondary fibre in the middle layer [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.6]
- Low-density tissue avoids compaction: pneumatic water removal, sheet enters dryer at about 25% consistency vs about 40% with pressing [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.7]
- Evaporative load roughly doubles per pound of fibre (3.0 lb vs 1.5 lb) but fibre weight per unit falls by as much as 30%, so extra drying cost is more than offset [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.7]
- Conventional Yankee press load is about 300 pli [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.7]
- Through-air-drying passes hot unsaturated gas through the porous wet web by pressure differential; TAD capacity grew 50% from 1996 to 2000 and 27% of North American tissue machines used through dryers [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.8]
