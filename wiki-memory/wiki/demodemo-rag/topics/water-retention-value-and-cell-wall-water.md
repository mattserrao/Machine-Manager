- WRV by centrifuge from about 10% solids; SCAN uses 3000 g vs TAPPI 900 g, removing more inter-fiber water; difference in mass divided by dry mass is reported [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.18]
- Fiber saturation point (large dextran exclusion) preferred for precise theory [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.18]
- Cell-wall water increases with refining and is "believed not to be removed ... to a significant extent in the forming section"; WRV correlates strongly with moisture left after high vacuum and after wet pressing [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.18]
- Jonsson and Jonsson model assumed 0.3 parts water per mass of cellulosic matter does not move relative to solids [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.18]
- Ultimate dryness set by surface tension of water in fiber pores (Kerekes and McDonald 2020) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.14]
- Refining is monitored by drainage tests (TAPPI T 227 om-94) yet lower freeness slows drainage-limited machines and wet pressing [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.17-18]
- Fiber sizes: softwood 3 to 4 mm long, 30 to 50 um wide; hardwood near 1 mm, 15 to 30 um; earlywood collapses more readily; juvenile wood shorter [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.17]
- Over 70% of headbox jet solids is cellulosic fiber; kraft delignification leaves mesopores and refining delaminates S2, making fibers more conformable [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.17]
- Conformability increases with decreasing yield; high-yield (TMP, high-yield kraft) pulps are stiffer and give higher dryness after pressing; recycled hornified fibers are less conformable [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.17]
- Refining occurs at 4 to 6% consistency between rotor and stator bars [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.17]
