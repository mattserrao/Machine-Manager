## Types
- Conical: rotating plug in a stator, bars lengthwise, plug position sets clearance; low-angle (Jordan) and high-angle (Claflin). [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.30]
- Disc: single disc, two opposing rotating discs (Fig 13-17), rotating double-sided disc between two stationary discs (Fig 13-18); flow parallel (duoflow) or series (monoflow) (Fig 13-19). [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.30]

## Disc advantages over conical
- Lower no-load energy, higher consistency, higher loading and speed, versatile plate design, self-correcting wear, smaller footprint, lower capital cost per ton. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.1]
- Caveat: at the same operating conditions conical and disc refiners develop fibers in a similar way. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.1]
- Floating disc: pressure equal on both faces, disc self-centres, energy splits equally, axial thrust balances so no thrust bearing needed. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.1]

## Gap, speed, wear
- Gap between plate faces sets the work at a given throughput; control tightly while avoiding plate contact ("clashing"). [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.1]
- "the refining gap typically falls between 0.1 mm and 1.0 mm, depending on stock consistency and throughput." [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.3]
- Higher disc speed lowers intensity for same throughput, but no-load power rises with the cube of rotor speed; most no-load loss is at the periphery. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.3]
- Typical peripheral speed about 4700 to 5700 fpm; maximum speed limited by disc diameter; Sunds added a conical section at the periphery. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.3]
- Wear patterns (Fig 13-20): worn disc plates mate more evenly after gap adjustment, unlike conicals. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.3]

## Plates
- Coarse bar patterns give high-intensity cutting; fine patterns build strength; Fig 13-21 shows five segments coarse to fine. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.4]
- Ni-hard (nickel-chromium white iron) is most widely used: abrasion resistant, edges stay sharp; unsuitable for corrosive stocks. Titanium and advanced plastics judged uneconomical. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.4]
- Wear is mainly abrasion, worse with foreign material; plate worn out when fiber quality or throughput falls below acceptable. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.4]

## Newer conicals
- Sunds ConFlo low-angle: speed not limited by circumferential speed, lower intensity, suits hardwood. Pilao (Brazil): wide-angle double-flow floating conical rotor. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.4]

## Energy by grade (Fig 13-22, Gilbert)
- Disc needs the least energy, Jordan the most, Claflin between; range about 1-3 hp-day/ton (deflaking, hot stock refining) to about 21-27 for greaseproof/glassine and release. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.5]
- Order low to high: deflaking, hot stock, filler board, deshive 9-pt waste, linerboard base/wrapping, tissue, bag and sack (bleached then unbleached), printing, bleached board/milk carton, cablewrap, fine papers, linerboard top, boxboard top liner, NSSC surface, release, greaseproof/glassine. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.5]
