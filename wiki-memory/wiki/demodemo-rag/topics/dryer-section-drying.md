## Performance criteria
- The dryer section is the most expensive part of the machine in capital and operating cost, mainly energy [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.7]
- Evaporation drying rate is lb water/h/ft2 of dryer surface (kg/h/m2); high is wanted only within grade and quality limits [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.7]
- Compare machines on the same grade using "lines of equivalent performance" (Fig 17-2) and TAPPI data sheets (Fig 17-3) [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.7]
- Steam economy: kJ/kg, or kg steam per kg water evaporated; "about 1.3 kg steam per kg water is typical of a modern, well-designed, well-maintained system. Many machines run noticeably higher." [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.7]
- The steam/condensate system and the air-handling system have the largest influence on energy consumption [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.7]
- Newsprint over 3000 ft/min: good operation is a two-tier two-felt dryer at 3500 ft/min with close-clearance syphons and effective pocket ventilation; same dryer with dryer bars gives lower rates at a given temperature (TAPPI TIS 0404-15) [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.8]

## Drying curve zones (Fig 17-4)
- Warm-up: first two or three cylinders mainly heat the sheet [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.8]
- Constant rate zone: free water evaporates at a steady, peak rate [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.8]
- Falling rate zone begins at about 9% moisture [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.8]
- Bound water zone: evaporation slows further; machines that over-dry to correct uniformity lose average rate [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.8]
- Wet web enters at 50-60% moisture (40-50% dryness); cylinders typically 60 or 72 inches diameter [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.8]
- Most machines have three to five independently felted dryer sections, each with its own speed control; sections also grouped for steam pressure control [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.8]

## Heat transfer
- Dryer pocket cycle: sheet picks up sensible heat on the cylinder, then flashes off steam and cools in the open draw [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.9]
- Main thermal resistances: condensate layer inside the cylinder and the air layer between sheet and cylinder; dirt film can add resistance, doctors keep surfaces clean [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.9]
- Air layer reduced by felt tension; beyond a certain tension no further reduction; required tension scales with machine speed and cylinder diameter^1.5 [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.9]
- Recommended newsprint felt tension is plotted in pli (0 to 12) vs speed 500-2500 fpm for 48, 60 and 72-inch dryers (Fig 17-8, TAPPI TIS 0404-04); bigger diameter needs higher tension [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.10]
- Non-condensibles (air) trapped in cylinders impair heat transfer and cause uneven drying [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.9]
- Condensate layer is the most significant resistance on high-speed machines [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.9]
- Steam condenses at the saturation temperature set by system pressure; steam is often supplied superheated to prevent pipeline condensation [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.9]

## Alternatives (not widely adopted)
- Air-impingement and infrared for coatings; airborne drying for pulp and extensible paper; through-air drying for tissue [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.15]
- Microwave/dielectric drying removes water selectively from wet streaks but "has not been widely adopted, probably because of unfavorable economics" [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.16]
- Press drying, impulse drying (drying rates claimed two to three orders of magnitude higher) not commercial; Condebelt commercial in the late 1990s [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.17]
