## Cost and strategy
- Estimated corrosion loss about $10 per tonne of product; roughly one-third judged preventable [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.2]
- Corrosion cannot be eliminated, goal is to keep it tolerable; anticipate in design, process and equipment choice, then monitor after start-up [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.2]
- Four principal control means: engineering design, materials selection, control of process variables, protection [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]

## Forms (Fig. 25-16)
- Classified by wet vs dry, mechanism (electrochemical or direct chemical), and appearance (uniform or localized) [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.2]
- Uniform corrosion is most common and easiest to measure and predict; localized corrosion is harder to predict and control [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.2]
- Fig. 25-16 forms: uniform, galvanic, erosion, fretting, crevice, pitting, exfoliation, selective leaching, intergranular, stress corrosion cracking, corrosion fatigue [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.2]
- Stress corrosion cracking = tensile stress plus corrosive environment; cannot be reliably predicted [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.2]
- Corrosion fatigue (cyclic stress) can fail at stress levels well below safe values, cracks can be invisible; examples include boilers, continuous digesters, paper machine suction rolls, refiner discs [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]
- Sulfur-using bacteria can promote corrosion as cathodic depolarizers; control may need biocides, surfactants, chlorinators [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]

## Testing and process variables
- Most common test is weight-loss coupons (typically 10 to 50 g); US rates reported in mils per year; results depend on surface finish, placement, duration [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]
- Table 25-4 variables: concentration of corrosive, pH, temperature, impurities, aeration, fluid velocity, inhibitors [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]
- Rule of thumb cited for the paper machine approach system: "corrosion rate roughly doubles for every 20 F rise in temperature" (paraphrased in notes) [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]
- Raising pH often reduces corrosivity; many metals resist alkalis but are attacked by acids [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]

## Protection
- Inhibitors (organic or inorganic; anodic or cathodic) work best in relatively closed systems [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]
- Cathodic protection applies an opposing current; anodic protection has worked on white and green liquor storage tanks and continuous digesters [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]
- Most corrosion-resistant alloys rely on thin passive oxide films; local breakdown causes pitting [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.3]
