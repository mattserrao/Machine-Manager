## What and why
- Refining is a physical treatment of pulp fibers to improve papermaking characteristics; essential for strong, smooth paper. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.2]
- Increases bonding area: collapses hollow walls into ribbons, fibrillates the surface, makes fibers flexible, cuts some long fibers to help formation. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.3]
- Refining removes the primary and S1 layers, exposing S2 (70-85% of fiber mass), which then delaminates and collapses. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.5]
- Action is shearing between two surfaces ("rolling friction"), stock at 2-5% consistency, multiple passes or single pass. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.8]
- Five sub-steps include wad gathering with dewatering to 15-30%, mechanical pressure of the order 1000 to 5000 psi, water expulsion of 50-60%. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.7]

## Cutting, bruising, splitting
- Cutting shortens fibers (short stiff fibers, bulky sheet) and reduces freeness; bruising keeps length and makes flexible fibers (high strength, low bulk). [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.9]
- Splitting = cutting plus bruising; most stock preparation involves splitting. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.10]

## Refiner equipment
- Rotating disk refiners: single-disk (one rotating, one stationary) or double-disk (one rotor with two sides plus two stators = two chambers). [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.18]
- Disk refiner advantages: most energy efficient design, higher consistencies, higher loads and RPM, compact and lower capital cost, easy to maintain, customisable plate patterns. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.23]
- Low flow problems: little or no fiber mat, channeling, high pressure rise (25-50 psi), plate crashing, short plate life, poor strength, more fines. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.24]
- High flow problems: compromised plate design, short plate life, high pressure drop, motors maxed out. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.25]

## Secondary fiber results
- Table 17.1 linerboard base sheet, virgin furnish mill refined (3.8% cons., 54" DD, 450 RPM): net 110 kWh/t, 3.6 Ws/m, freeness 745 to 655 ml, tensile index 38.9 to 64.6 (+66.1%), tear index 20.8 to 13.5. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.37]
- Same table, OCC furnish lab refined (3.6% cons., 20" DD, 900 RPM): net 30 kWh/t, 1.6 Ws/m, freeness 610 to 530 ml, tensile 29.8 to 38.8 (+30.2%), burst +61.3%. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.37]
- Deinked ledger grades: main strength problem is high ash giving low freeness after repulping. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.38]
- "Washing DI pulp increases freeness but have low tensile. Refining DI pulp increases tensile but low freeness. Refining and then washing DI pulp gives good tensile and freeness." [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.39]
- LC refining is used in deinking lines for SC paper (Fig. 153, refiner in the PM loop). [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.17]
