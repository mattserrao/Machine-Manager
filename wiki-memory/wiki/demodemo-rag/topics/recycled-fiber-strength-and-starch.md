## Why strength drops
- Reduced inter-fiber bonding from: fines build-up, hornification of the cell wall and fiber surface, accumulated non-fibrous contaminants (clay, ink, stickies), multivalent cations bonding to carboxyl groups, and limited refining because of freeness [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.16]
- "There is a constant compromise between cleanliness, yield of wastepaper, and strength development" [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.17]
- Recycled fibers drain slower (higher fines); board grades have high basis weights so drainage limits cut productivity [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.13]
- Vat formers: inert recycled fines hinder ply bonding; removing them cuts yield but helps [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.14]
- Printing and writing: recycled can have similar strength at lower freeness, same intrinsic fiber strength, similar tear; anionic trash and lower bonding lower strength [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.59]

## Fixes
- Increase basis weight, use strength aids (starch, very widely used in Europe, less in the USA), higher grades of fiber [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.17]
- Starch is the most important dry strength additive in brown grades, can be >5% of final sheet weight; also improves surface strength, pick resistance, inter-ply bonding [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.18]
- Starch downsides: more drying load (size press can limit speed at fixed drying capacity), higher effluent organic load, dirtier machines, harder wet end chemistry, can impair internal sizing and formation [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.19]
- Corrugating medium: starch at the size press or more wet pressing; porosity is an indicator of starch uptake; lower starch viscosity helps absorption but may mean reducing machine speed [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.22]
- Test liner improvements: starch or chemicals, higher basis weight, more mechanical pressing, fractionation, grade selection [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.23]
- Box-board: stiffness is the key property; raise via refining or starch; calendering smooths but reduces caliper, stiffness and compressive strength [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.21]
- Printing and writing: blend with virgin, improved processing, "Increased amounts of cationic starch" (strength and anionic trash collector), proper refining [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.66]

## Grade limits
- Virgin kraft liner can take filler fibers up to about 15-20%; up to 20% wastepaper still classes as virgin liner in the USA [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.8]
- Corrugated stiffness is why hardwood fibers are used in medium [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.4]
- Linerboard machines are fourdriniers; newer ones make two-ply via secondary headbox, two wires or multilayer headbox; twin wire gap formers are being used [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.15]
- Recycled vs virgin newsprint: higher density, lower caliper, more absorbent, lower friction coefficient, strength generally equivalent, brightness lower [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.29]
