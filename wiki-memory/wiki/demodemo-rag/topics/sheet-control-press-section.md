## Definitions
- Drop-off: sheet rides on the bottom of the fabric from pick-up roll to first nip; at start-up or later from edge contamination or compaction [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.1]
- Sheet stealing: sheet attracted to the wrong felt in a double-felted nip, or not released from a single-felted nip and carried to uhle boxes [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.1]
- Edge flipping: stealing-type edge lift in a clustered press (off the center roll) or into a double-felted press as air moves to the edge [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.2]
- Blowing: common on last press of lightweight fine paper and news at high speed; sheet lifts from the felt before the nip and wrinkles; can also appear late in felt life when a felt gets too closed and defeats anti-blowing equipment [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.2]

## Key drivers
- Surface attraction (denier, roll surface energy, water film), film splitting, air movement, nip loading, water split [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.3]
- Secondary: sheet moisture into the nip, freeness, felt moisture and permeability differences, conditioning, felt design and wear, exit geometry, vented roll design, doctor condition, Ra, steambox profile [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.3]
- Upstream: impingement and sealing can cause long MD streaks and localised CD bands [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.4]
- Former exit solids: 1% change is nearly 10% change in gpm to the first nip; trim cut quality varies when exit solids sit near 15-16% [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.5] [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.6]

## Trim squirts and transfer set-up
- Squirt supply must be good quality and deaerated; jets laminar at the right pressure for sheet speed; angle jet slightly outward to limit wet mounding at the edge, a few degrees downstream to avoid debris on the nozzle [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.6]
- Pickup suction roll penetration about 0.5 in (12.5 mm), even front to back; wrap 4-6 in; vacuum split into high and holding zone, holding vacuum adjustable [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.7]
- Draw between wire and pickup felt "can be low, but they need to be positive"; pickup felt speed differs from nip speed [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.8]
- Felt gap into the nip: about 0.75 in (19 mm) typically adequate; sandwiching blocks air in, some air channel is needed at higher speed [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.8]
- Transfer boxes: usually two slots; felt separates over the first slot and sheet held at the second; transfer suction rolls become a necessity at higher speeds and often need the unused compartment blocked (HDPE strip) [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.13] [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.14]
- Bubbles can persist until a draw or felt geometry change vents them; separation point can move after a grade change, new felt, or not returning to optimum set-up after outages [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.14]

## Tri-nip and center roll
- Sheet follows the felt/roll last wrapped (exit angle); OEM wrap guidelines for the center roll; more wrap helps attachment but adds rewet [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.11] [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.16]
- Center roll Ra should be profiled in CD at regular intervals; too smooth raises draw and plugs the doctor, too rough makes the felt more attractive and steals the sheet; shower water passing the doctor makes release harder [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.17]
- High recycled/stickies content shears out at the doctor; change blades more often and clean holders [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.17]

## Felt design and conditioning
- Finer-denier cap on the felt the sheet should follow, 1-2 steps finer than the opposite felt; finer cap plugs and wears more easily; longer felt cycles shrink the window for cap choice [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.19]
- Uhle box vacuum below about 16-18 in Hg (54-61 kPa); active vacuum control above 18 in Hg (61 kPa); uhle box lube showers need coverage, not volume [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.19] [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.24]
- Edges are harder to condition: use double nozzles or extra nozzles at half spacing on HP showers and keep uhle box covers at the edges [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.21]
- Felt permeability falls through the cycle; felts with finer batt or higher batt-to-base ratio start and plateau lower and need more careful chemical cleaning [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.22]
- Example data: last-press felt vacuum fell after the first third of the cycle and roughness rose with felt days (Figs 27-28, R-sq 16% and 22.5%) [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.20]

## Lange transfer rules (all else equal, sheet follows)
- 1 plain roll/belt over felt; 3 the wetter felt; 4 the older felt; 5 lowest permeability; 6 finest batt; 7 smoothest; 8 the felt that wraps the exit roll last; 9 felt with suction roll behind; 13 direction of greatest dewatering; 14 sticks harder to a heated roll [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.25]
- "multiple and contradictory rules apply" in any real case [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.25]

## Diagnosis approach
- Issues are usually intermittent when equipment and felt design are sound; note when in the felt cycle and on which grade, then use DMAIC or Kepner-Tregoe for chronic ones [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.24]
- Fishbone for sheet edge flipping groups causes: rolls, chemical, equipment, clothing, conditioning, furnish, methods [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.26]
- Special cases: biofilm/chemical films on felts, enzyme trial byproducts, pH shifts in batch cleaning, saveall pan overflow, old steambox distributors, felt tension [DOC 39e95278-beb1-4937-867a-db98ca39f611 p.23]
