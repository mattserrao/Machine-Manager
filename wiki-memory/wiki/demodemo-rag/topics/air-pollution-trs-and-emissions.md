## Regulation and sources
- Manufacturing accounts for only about 20% of measurable air pollution in North America [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.22]
- Detection threshold for most reduced sulfur gases is about 1 ppb [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.22]
- US basis: 1970 Clean Air Act; 1990 amendments use Maximum Achievable Control Technology (MACT) for hazardous air pollutants [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.22]
- MACT for existing sources reflects the best-controlled 12% of sources; for new sources the single best; 1997 standards for pulping, bleaching, recovery furnaces, smelt tanks, lime kilns, wastewater; 2000 for power boilers [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.23]
- Table 27-1: fine particulates (soda fume, recovery furnace); coarse particulates (fly ash, hog fuel and coal boilers); sulfur oxides (sulfite mills); NOx (all combustion); reduced sulfur gases (kraft); VOCs (digester relief, evaporation) [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.23]
- NOx can be controlled in recovery and power boilers and lime kilns by lower flame temperature and limited excess air [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.23]

## TRS
- TRS = hydrogen sulfide, methyl mercaptan, dimethyl sulfide, dimethyl disulfide; main sources are digester blow and relief gases, evaporator noncondensibles, recovery boiler exhaust [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.24]
- Table 27-2 odour thresholds: H2S about 1 ppb (rotten eggs); methyl mercaptan about 1 ppb; dimethyl sulfide and disulfide about 10 ppb [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.24]
- Table 27-3 TRS (ppm by volume): batch digester blow 100-75,000; batch relief 350-180,000; continuous digester 3,000-24,000; washer hood vents 0-30; evaporator hotwell 2,500-36,000; recovery furnace after DCE 5-2,000; smelt dissolving tank 0-80; lime kiln 0-450; slake vent 0-25 [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.24]
- Bleach plant is a minor source (chlorine and chlorine dioxide); scrubbed with cold water, dilute caustic or extraction effluent [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.24]

## Recovery boiler guidelines (27.4)
- Early-1970s guidelines: furnace loading not above about 115% of rated solids (older boilers); primary air at most 65% of total; excess oxygen 2.0-2.5% (TRS rises below 2%); sulfidity below 30%; firing liquor solids at least 62% [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.28]
- Black liquor oxidation only matters where direct-contact evaporators remain; low-odour boilers drop the direct-contact evaporator [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.28]
- Noncondensibles are combined into one header, usually burned in the lime kiln; explosion hazard handled by flame arresters and rupture discs (Fig. 27-11) [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.28] [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.29]

## Monitoring (27.3)
- Source sampling is the minimum requirement; testing at stable operation misses upsets [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.24]
- Manual particulate tests need isokinetic sampling (Fig. 27-3); Table 27-4 lists seven reasons for source sampling [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.25]
- CEMs exist for TRS, SO2, NOx, hydrocarbons, CO2, CO; no fully satisfactory particulate CEM yet; newer gas CEMs claim up to six months between maintenance [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.25]
- Ambient monitoring typically at least 8 stations sited by prevailing wind; dustfall in tons per square mile per month; high-volume sampler 24 h in ug/m3; corrosion coupons in mg/cm2/month [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.26] [DOC e24788a9-3652-41e0-a461-6f09e5cc575c p.27]
