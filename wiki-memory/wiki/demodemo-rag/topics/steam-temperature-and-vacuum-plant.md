## Superheated vs saturated
- Saturated steam is better for heat exchangers, yet "about 90% of all paper machines use overheated steam" because of the steam and condensate system construction [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.5]
- Dryer = tube heat exchanger; condensate moves with the dryer above 350 m/min and is pressed into a ring above 500 m/min (ring about 6-10 mm; dryer diameter ca. 1450-1800 mm) [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.5]
- Steam usually is not generated at machine quality: pressure reduction with steam cooling between power house and PM; about 10 C over saturation at the main distributor; about 95% of machines have this distribution [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.6]

## First dryers and heat-up curve
- With 3 drying groups, group 1 is heated much lower than group 3; paper moisture at about 45-50 C is warmed before evaporation starts in groups 2 and 3 [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.7]
- Individual control: each first-group dryer has its own steam pressure valve plus a differential pressure valve on the condensate side to set a heat-up curve [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.7]
- Setpoints: first dryer lowest, next about 0,2-0,3 bar higher; light paper (newspaper) needs 50 C to 65 C at the first dryer [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.8]
- Berlin: the real surface heat-up curve comes from decreasing paper cooling, not from low steam temperature; "it is not possible to reduce the steam temperature by means of two or three pressure reductions from 165 C ... to 100 C or even 90 C ... only possible by using steam cooling" [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.8]

## Vacuum plant
- Needed only if dryers must be heated with steam below 100 C, i.e. below 0,1 bar abs; first dryers or dryers after the size press are often below 1,0 bar abs [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.10]
- Berlin recommends steam coolers instead of vacuum plants in many cases: low pressure does not give the low temperature [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.10]
- Components shown: vacuum pump, separation vessel, auxiliary condenser (45 C / 22 C cooling water in the figure), vent valve [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.10]

## Ventilation and start-up
- After a stop, steam condenses and air is sucked in through flanges or open drain valves until pipes and dryers are full of air [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.11]
- Air is pushed out by inflowing steam: about half an hour for a machine with 35 dryers; vacuum pump faster but not needed [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.11]
- Cast-steel dryers must be heated slowly: 1 to 2 hours after cooling down [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.11]
