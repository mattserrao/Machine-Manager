## Classes
- Mechanical and semichemical are generally separated at about 85% yield [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.2]
- Fig 5-2 family: pure mechanical CSF 50-200 yield 93-96%; low sulfonation CSF 60-700 yield 90-93%; chemimechanical CSF 50-700 yield 85-93% [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.1]
- TMP: presteam above 100 C, first stage above 100 C, second stage atmospheric; PGW: grinding above 100 C [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.1]
- Shift from SGW: 90% of North American mechanical pulp in 1975, refiner methods over 50% by 1990 [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.30]

## Stone groundwood (SGW)
- Stone peripheral speed 25 to 36 m/s; up to six grinders each driven by a 9000 kW motor on a large plant [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.2]
- Logs enter at about 45% moisture; minimum wood moisture 30%, preferred 45% to 50% [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.2, p.6]
- Abrasive layer about 7 cm, stone replaced after about two years; Al2O3 or SiC grit in vitrified bond [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.3]
- Grit: smaller gives low-freeness groundwood, intermediate newsgrade, coarse board grade [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.3]
- Freeness is the main process control parameter; operator variables are magazine pressure and sharpening cycle [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.4]
- Over a sharpening cycle specific energy, freeness and production fall while burst/tensile rise; a freshly sharpened stone initially lowers quality [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.4]
- Table 5-3 newsprint: 60-70 hp-day/ton, CSF 85-95; board: 50-60 hp-day/ton, CSF 160-175; tissue 40-130, CSF 75-140 [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.4]
- Burr about 7.5 cm x 7.5 cm, target groove depth about 1.6 mm, new burr each sharpening; Valmet Water Jet uses 500-2400 bar, about 130 grinders by early 2001 [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.5]
- Too much shower water hinders softening; too little allows charring [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.4]
- Table 5-4 (energy MJ/kg, burst, tear, brightness): spruce 5.0, 1.4, 4.1, 61; balsam 5.2, 1.3, 3.8, 59; hemlock 5.6, 1.1, 3.2, 57; pine 6.3, 0.9, 2.8, 56 [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.6]

## Pressurized groundwood (PGW)
- About 30 psig overpressure, shower water above 95 C; tear 40-60% higher and tensile 20-30% higher than SGW; needs logs not chips [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.7]
- PGW-S: shower water returned at up to 140 C, stronger but more energy and slightly lower brightness [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.8]

## Refiner mechanical pulping (RMP) and plates
- RMP commercial since 1960; two high-consistency stages in series; discs up to 70 in (about 1.8 m), drive up to 43,000 HP (32 MW) [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.8]
- Latency: pulp must be held in hot water to remove curl before freeness can be measured [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.9]
- Plates: breaker bars near the eye, progressively finer bars in the refining zone, taper 0.01 mm/mm or less, dams and closed periphery to raise residence time [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.9]
- Control: run at 18% to 30% consistency; hold motor load/throughput/discharge consistency via plate pressure, feed rate and dilution water; hold specific energy via load or throughput [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.10]
- Steam must exhaust freely; uneven exhaust causes "blowback" and load swings, best controlled by lowering feed temperature and consistency [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.11]
- Chips: washing protects plates; compression wood and odd fragments cause feeding and heat-penetration problems [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.11]

## TMP
- Table 5-6 SGW / RMP / TMP: energy 5.0 / 6.4 / 7.0 MJ/kg; burst 1.4 / 1.9 / 2.3; tear 4.1 / 7.5 / 9.0; shives 3.0 / 2.0 / 0.5%; brightness 61.5 / 59 / 58.5% [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.11]
- Steaming heats chips to about 90 C; prolonged steaming causes thermal darkening; air must be removed [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.13]
- Temperature windows: below 140 C fibres coated with lignin that turns glassy; above 140 C fibres separate easily at low energy; 120-130 C secondary-wall fractures [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.13]
- Blowlines between stages: too small gives pressure drop, too large lets fibre deposit on walls [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.13]
- Andritz RTS and Metso Thermopulp claim 15-20% energy saving versus conventional TMP [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.14]

## Chemically modified (CTMP, CMP, APMP)
- Sulphonation softening is permanent, steam softening temporary [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.14]
- CTMP: sodium sulfite 1% to 5% on oven-dry wood; about 1% suits newsprint furnish [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.15]
- Table 5-7: mechanical reject rates typically 25-35% (sometimes over 50%); chemimechanical 15-25% [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.17]
- CMP yield usually 85-90%, used as reinforcement pulp in newsprint [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.17]
- APMP (caustic + peroxide) benefits most for low-density hardwoods such as aspen and poplar; chemical impregnation needs air-free chips (atmospheric presteaming) [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.17]
