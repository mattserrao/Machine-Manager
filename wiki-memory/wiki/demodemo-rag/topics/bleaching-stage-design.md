Source: [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-07]] pp.3-7. Sequences: [[bleaching-sequences-and-stages]].

- Main stage variables: chemical application, chemical consumption, consistency, temperature, reaction time, pH [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.3]
- A stage runs from chemical addition to washing: steam mixing, chemical mixing, reactor, wash [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.3]
- Poor mixing: low chemical pick-up gives low brightness and shives; overbleaching hurts fiber strength [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.4]
- Peg mixers (to 1980s) are being superseded by high-shear mixers; ozone needs especially high mixing and a small vessel [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.4]
- Towers: retention "from 30 minutes to several hours"; consistency typically above 10% [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.5]
- Series of upflow towers must all run together; downflow towers allow variable retention and surge capacity [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.5]
- Gas-releasing stages (for example D or E+O) need at least one upflow leg to keep chemical under hydraulic pressure [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.7]
- Washing: countercurrent filtrate reuse, dilution factor about 2 typical, net flow through the sheet must be positive; chloride build-up drives corrosion so modern plants use resistant metallurgy [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.7]
- "Closing up": extended and oxygen delignification cut bleach chemical use by about two-thirds; goal of totally effluent-free mill [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.7]
- Displacement bleaching (Rapson and Anderson 1966; Kamyr diffusion washers) displaces chemical through the mat so inter-stage washing is unnecessary; recent designs use fewer stages per tower and a separate tower for final D [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.12]
