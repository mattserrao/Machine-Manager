## History and liquor cycle
- Soda process precursor patented 1854, first soda mill 1866; kraft credited to C.F. Dahl, patent 1884, first use Sweden 1885 [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.29]
- White liquor = NaOH + Na2S; black liquor burned in recovery furnace; smelt (Na2CO3 + Na2S) dissolved to green liquor; lime (CaO) causticizes back to white liquor, lime mud CaCO3 byproduct [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.29]
- Recovery steps: weak liquor about 15% solids evaporated to heavy liquor 70-75% solids, burned, smelt dissolved, causticized [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.2]
- Recovery detail: [[black-liquor-oxidation]], [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-06]]

## Terms (Table 7-1, g/L as Na2O)
- "Active alkali (AA): NaOH + Na2S"; "Effective alkali (EA): NaOH + 1/2 Na2S"; TTA = NaOH + Na2S + Na2CO3 [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.30]
- "Sulfidity: Na2S / AA (or / TTA), % on Na2O basis. The basis must be stated each time." [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.30]
- Causticity = NaOH/(NaOH + Na2CO3); reduction efficiency often Na2S/(Na2S + Na2SO4) [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.30]
- Mills keep white liquor sulfidity 25-35% (TTA basis); below about 15% cook rate and quality are hurt; benefit of sulfide marginal above about 15% [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.30] [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.6]
- Fig 7-2 converts AA to EA by sulfidity (0-40%); at a given AA, EA falls as sulfidity rises [DOC 12a3bba4-9144-478f-9140-9e24c40c2051 p.30]

## Cooking
- Batch: max temperature typically about 170 C after 1.0-1.5 h, held up to 2 h [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.1]
- Continuous: intermediate zone 115-120 C for penetration, hold 1-1.5 h at peak, quench below 100 C [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.2]
- Typical bleachable cook dissolves about 80% of lignin, 50% of hemicelluloses, 10% of cellulose [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.2]
- In 155-175 C the delignification rate "more than doubles for each 10 C rise" [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.2]
- H-factor (Vroom): relative rate 1 at 100 C; area under rate-vs-time curve; Fig 7-4 rate reaches about 900 at plateau [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.3]
- Residual lignin on wood at end of cook: softwood 4-5%, hardwood about 3% [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.2]
- Cooking above 180 C avoided: viscosity (G-factor) and strength losses [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.4]
- About 90% of extractives removed early; tall oil soap and turpentine recovered [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.4]
- Prehydrolysis (dissolving grades): yield 5-7% lower; Fig 7-9 total cycle about 480 min incl. 120 min prehydrolysis and 70 min kraft cook at about 170 C [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.5]

## Control variables
- Goal: consistent kappa (or permanganate number); Table 7-2 variables: chips, sulfidity, charge as AA/EA on od wood, liquor-to-wood ratio, temperature cycle, H-factor [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.4]
- Effective alkali: softwood about 12-14% on dry wood, hardwood 8-10%; higher alkali needs lower H-factor for same kappa [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.6]
- Liquor-to-wood ratio typically 3 to 5; batch digester about 75% full at start [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.6]
- Penetration: lengthwise 50-200x faster than across grain for non-swelling liquids, about 6x with NaOH, so chip thickness matters; 5 mm chips give far fewer screenings than 7 mm (Fig 7-10) [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.5]
- Pin chips, fines and sawdust lower yield, strength and raise alkali use [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.5]
- Optimum kappa for bleached yield about 28-34 in the Fig 7-13 example [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.7]

## Modifications
- AQ: about 1 lb per ton pulp, 1-2% yield gain on dry wood; in 1998 about one-third of US kraft mills used it [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.7]
- Polysulfide: sulfur 3-5% on wood; modern methods oxidise white liquor sulfide instead of adding sulfur; AQ and polysulfide gains add [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.8]
- Extended delignification (Sweden, early 1980s): softwood kappa 12-15 versus about 30 conventional; costs longer cooks, some yield loss, recovery furnace load [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.8]
- Oxygen delignification dates from the 1960s, common in North America [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.8]
