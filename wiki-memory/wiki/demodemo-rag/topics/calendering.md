- Most grades calendered mainly for a smooth printing surface; compaction is a disadvantage for foodboard and tissue; for newsprint thickness reduction is wanted [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.17]
- Replication of the roll surface improves with pressure, shear, heat or moisture; dwell time in the nip is extremely short [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.17]
- On-machine calendering preferred; off-machine supercalendering is costly; on-machine soft-nip calenders are replacing supercalenders for less critical grades [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.17]
- Hard-nip calenders give uniform thickness but turn basis-weight variation into density and print mottle [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.18]
- Supercalenders (hard roll with soft roll) give more uniform density; basis-weight variation shows as thickness variation [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.18]
- First two wrapped rolls in the stack should be heated; king roll (bottom) needs variable crown; queen roll (second from bottom) is usually the driven roll [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.18]
- Soft-nip: two nips used for equal finish on both sides; resilient roll usually water-cooled; all rolls individually driven so nips can open and close at machine speed [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.18]
- Moisture strongly affects compressibility; methods: sweat roll, steam or mist showers, water boxes; breaker stack calenders between the last two dryer sections at higher moisture [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.19]
- Iron-roll temperature by internal fluid or external induction heating; induction is more versatile; tri-pass drilled roll most efficient, bored roll least [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.19]
- Long-nip (shoe) calendering widens the nip instead of raising pressure; only one shoe calender operating in 2000 [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.19]
- Induction coil sits with a 3 mm gap to the roll surface (Honeywell, Fig 17-35) [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.20]

## Supercalendering (Smook 18.3, from [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-11]])
- Stand-alone supercalender is a vertical stack of alternating hard metal rolls and soft compressed-fibre rolls, unwind stand at top, rewind at bottom; lead rolls stop air being trapped (creasing) [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.10]
- Soft roll deforms under load and creeps, so its surface slides on the steel roll; the rolling friction polishes and smooths [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.11]
- Unlike a hard-nip machine calender, about 25% of drive power goes into roll heating; controlled temperature rolls now use the heat deliberately [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.11]
- Single-finishing stack finishes only the side against the steel rolls; a reversing nip in the middle gives a double-finishing stack with similar finish on both sides; blow rolls cool the sheet and prevent wrinkling [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.11]
- Traditional soft rolls: cotton and other cellulosic fibre, up to 25% wool; sensitive to marking, damaged rolls must be replaced at once [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.12]
- Off-machine supercalendering limited to about 750 m/min; two or sometimes three supercalenders needed per paper machine [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.12]
- Polymer-covered soft rolls since the early 1990s: fewer roll changes, less grinding, higher temperature, speed and nip loading; cotton-filled rolls no longer supplied for new installations [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.12]
- Voith Janus MK2 (started 1999) first on-machine supercalender of its type, eight rolls at 45 degrees, supercalendered grades at over 1500 m/min [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.12]
- For coated sheets, high supercalender pressure aligns kaolin platelets near the sheet plane and raises gloss; uneven coating becomes more uneven and adhesive-rich areas give a mottled look [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.10]
- Multiply board: calendering is a major cause of plybond loss, see [[multiply-paperboard]] [DOC 8b9baf40-fc3e-4d72-974a-2d9e679268cc p.22]
