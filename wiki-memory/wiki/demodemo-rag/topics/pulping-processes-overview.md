## Classes and yield
- Table 4-1 yields: mechanical 85-95% (short, weak fibres, good print quality); hybrid 55-85%; chemical 40-55% (long strong fibres, poor print quality) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.21]
- Yield = oven-dry pulp weight divided by oven-dry wood weight [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.21]
- Chemical is about 75% of North American production, about 97% of that kraft; semichemical about 5% [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.21]
- Table 4-2 (1999, million short tons): USA total 66,000 (bleached kraft 32,800); Canada total 25,000 (bleached kraft 11,600, mechanical 11,000) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.21]
- Table 4-7 yield / strength rank (softwood): SGW 90-95% / 5; TMP 90% / 6-7; NSSC 65-80%; high-yield kraft 50-70% / 7; kraft 40-50% / 10; sulfite 45-55% / 9 [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.29]

## Cooking conditions (Table 4-4)
- Kraft: NaOH + Na2S, 2-4 h, pH 13+, 170-180 C [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.23]
- Acid sulfite: 4-20 h, pH 1-2, 120-135 C [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.23]
- Bisulfite: 2-4 h, pH 3-5, 140-160 C [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.23]
- NSSC: Na2SO3 + Na2CO3, 1/4-1 h, pH 7-9, 160-180 C [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.23]
- Kraft pros: highest strength, proven recovery, many species, tolerates bark; sulfite pros: brighter, bleaches easier, higher bleached yield, easier to refine (Table 4-5) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.24]
- No new sulfite mills built in North America since the 1960s [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.23]
- Kraft yield effect (Fig 4-7): strengths peak near 45-50% yield; folding endurance falls to zero near 80% yield [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.27]
- Semichemical yield 55-85%; pulp near 75% yield is ideal for the fluted corrugating medium; spent liquor via cross-recovery with kraft or fluidized bed incineration [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.24, p.25]
- Lower-yield pulps need more refining energy (more PFI revolutions) to reach 500 CSF (Table 4-6) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.26]

## Market and special pulps
- Market pulps supply about 11% of world fibre demand; non-paper pulps are under 5% of virgin pulp but over 20% of market pulp [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.26]
- Fluff pulp: moisture controlled at about 8-10%; high drying load is why many market mills avoid it [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.28]
- Dissolving pulp feeds viscose (xanthate route), cellophane, acetate; cotton linters 99% alpha cellulose [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.27]
- Drying makes fibres stiffer, paper bulkier and stronger in tear but weaker in burst and tensile than never-dried pulp [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.29]

## Trends
- Organosolv: Alcell (300 tpd plant abandoned for economics), Organocell (viable at 250 tpd vs about 1000 tpd for kraft) [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.28]
- Biopulping: two-week fungal pre-treatment gives about 30% energy saving; brightness reversion is a problem [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.28]
- CTMP and CMP are the fastest growing virgin pulps [DOC cb6a70a2-a00a-4244-809b-d0effdcde3f1 p.28]
