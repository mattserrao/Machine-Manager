## Nips
- Common wisdom: do not send a too-wet web into a press nip at high loading; Pikulik (1992) pressed newsprint at incoming solids "as low as 9.6%" but this "only works for lightweight sheets where the water volumes are low and can be handled by the felts" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.54]
- Single-felted nips produce a stratified sheet (densified felt side); alternate the side facing the felt in successive nips where the layout allows [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.54]
- High basis weight: double-felted nips (Kawka 1977), then one or two single-felted nips for higher maximum pressure; felt water-holding capacity must match removable water [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.54]
- Smoothing press (felt-less nip after the last felted nip) is proposed to reduce density stratification, but "pilot machine trials suggest otherwise (McDonald 2020)" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.54]
- Trend to extended-nip presses, especially for paperboard weights; optimise with ramps of increasing pressure and lower peak pressure than ordinary wet presses (Cedra 1999) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.54]
- Ultra-long pulses up to one second (Jarvinen 2018, 2019): 1000 ms vs 20 ms pulses gave 3 to 4% higher solids in water forming, more in foam forming; "no theoretical explanation" yet and not practical for traditional machines [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.55]

## Water leaving the felt
- Felt void space shrinks greatly in the nip; press rolls are drilled or grooved to take the filtered water; vacuum-drilled rolls become less effective with speed (water acceleration outward opposes the vacuum) and have strength and corrosion problems [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.55]

## Felt permeability and surface
- Press felts are highly permeable, but voids fill with pitch, minerals and chemicals; continual or periodic felt cleaning programs are used; felt replacement timing can be optimised with models (Drummond 2009) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.55]
- Transverse flow in the felt relieves hydraulic pressure buildup in the nip entrance [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.55]
- Large felt pores draw water back to the paper after maximum compression (capillary), limited by viscous resistance; Lucas-Washburn predicts slower rewet into a densified sheet [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.55] [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.56]
- Finer filaments in the felt surface layer reduce water at the paper-felt interface and so separation rewetting [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.53]
- Retention aids keep pitch tied to fibers so less is driven into the felt [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.59]
