Source: [[doc-hubbe-rate-limiting-water-removal]] (p.50-51). Equipment (Deculator, Pomp pump): [[approach-system-and-deaeration]]. Related: [[dewatering-enhancement-strategies]].

- Brecht and Kirchner (1959): about 0.75% air by volume in furnish increased handsheet dewatering time by a factor of more than two [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Rauch and Sangle (2000): two-fold increase in dewatering time in pilot experiments at 0.4% air [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Helle (2000): 0.8% air by volume approximately doubled the vacuum time needed to reach the same solids as air-free stock [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Lamminen (2004) found a decrease in drainage rate with air, "but the effect was small" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Typical headbox air (dissolved plus entrained) "can be up to 4% on a volume basis, once the pressure is released at the headbox slice" (Lorz 1987); released air forms tiny bubbles that plug drainage channels like fines [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Foam forming exception: even with 50 to 70% air content, dewatering rates in the same range as air-free systems were achieved (Lehmonen 2020) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Equipment: older vacuum deaerators on hydrocyclone accepts; newer centrifugal rotating-cylinder unit on white water (Helle 1999) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51]
- Most machines also use a defoamer (emulsion of water-insoluble surfactant) which lets small bubbles coalesce and pop; without mechanical deaeration, entrained air still suppresses drainage [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.51]
