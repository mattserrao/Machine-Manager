Source: [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-07]] pp.8-9. Supply: [[chlorine-dioxide-generation]]. Sequences: [[bleaching-sequences-and-stages]].

- Dose rule: "ClO2 dose = 0.1 x unbleached kappa number (% ClO2 on pulp)", about 80% of full chemical demand (notes) [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.9]
- ClO2 has about 2.5 times the oxidizing power of Cl2 per mole (2.63 per pound); 5 electrons vs 2 [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.9]
- Delignification: low temperature (under 20 C) and long retention preferred for shives; each 10 C doubles rate; about 25 C enough; optimum pH about 2-4 [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.9]
- Caustic extraction: 12-15% consistency, 60-80 C, up to 2 h, final pH above 10.8, NaOH about 50% of the ClO2 charge [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.9]
- ClO2 brightening: 11-14% consistency, 70-75 C, 2-4 h retention; terminal pH 3.5-4.0 first stage, 5.5-6.0 second stage; residual neutralized with SO2 or NaOH [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.9]
- ClO2 first used commercially in 1946; standard for high-brightness kraft by the late 1950s [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.9]
- 1990s dioxin concerns led to total replacement of chlorine with ClO2 [DOC d9815fa5-40a2-474b-9932-eaf665991fc0 p.8]
- Ozone (DZ) or xylanase can reduce ClO2 need: [[peroxide-ozone-enzyme-bleaching]]
