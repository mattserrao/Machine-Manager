- Web enters the press at about 20% solids; mechanical water removal is much cheaper than evaporation. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.23]
- Dryness % understates moisture change: 40 to 41% is 2.5% relative, but water-to-fibre ratio 1.50 to 1.44 (about 4.0%). [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.23]
- Four phases: (1) compression, air escapes, no hydraulic pressure; (2) saturated, hydraulic pressure drives water into felt to mid-nip; (3) nip opens until hydraulic pressure is zero (max dryness); (4) expansion, negative pressure, rewetting. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.23] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.24]
- Separate sheet and felt right after the nip to limit rewetting. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.24]
- Flow-limited nip: water cannot leave fast enough; excess pressure there causes sheet crushing; last nip is limited by peak pressure and residence time; water removal tied to nip impulse. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.24]
- Lineal nip pressure = (mechanical load + top roll weight) / contact length. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.25]
- Hard cover peak about 3300 kPa vs soft about 2000 kPa at 45 kN/m (Fig 16-61). [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.26]
- Crown about 1.3 mm at mid-roll; fixed crown fits one load; controlled-crown (swimming, Beloit, Nipco) shift deflection to a fixed shaft; zone-controlled rolls correct CD moisture streaks, steam showers are more sensitive. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.26] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.27]
- Lateral water travel: plain press about 20 mm, suction about 5 mm, grooved 1.3 mm; grooves 2.5 mm deep x 0.5 mm wide, 3.2 mm pitch. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.27]
- Blind-drilled rolls: more void volume, softer covers, self-cleaning. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.28]
- Double felting: first position for flow-limited presses, 2nd/3rd for sheets above 130 g/m2. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.28]
- Shoe press 1981; 4100 kPa over 25 cm shoe, about 1050 kN/m (first design); latest about 1500 kN/m; pressure lasts up to about 8x longer, lower peak, less crushing risk; over 400 in service by 2001. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.28] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.29]
- Open-draw transfer remains on older light machines below about 600 m/min; suction pickup since 1954; straight-through vs transfer press. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.30]
