## Designs
- Twinver press: draw between first and second press removed; wire side contacts one smooth roll across two nips (Fig 16-81) [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.1]
- No-draw presses developed for high speed, supporting lightweight sheets through the pressing sequence; sheet marking and suction roll failures on wide machines led to separate rolls for first and second nips [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.1]
- One-sided dewatering in triple-nip no-draw presses can cause two-sidedness (coating/ink uptake); an added open-draw fourth press is "now out of favor because of runnability problems" [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.1]
- Closed-transfer to dryers: a belt wraps the last hard centre roll and carries the web to the first dryer fabric, avoiding felt rewetting [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.1]
- TEM-SEC press: centre roll up to 3 m diameter steam-heated up to 3 bar, nip loads 80 to 280 kN/m [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.2]

## Hot pressing
- Sheet temperature 60 to 90 C; "every 10 C rise in sheet temperature adds about 1 percentage point of dryness after the last press nip" [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.2]
- Steam showers apply steam to one face with suction on the other; segmented zones allow profile correction [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.2]

## Nip and performance
- Lightweight grades dewater best in narrow hard nips; heavy grades in wide soft nips; press performance drops as speed rises [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.4]
- Table 16-1 variables: roll hardness and diameter, configuration, nip type; furnish, freeness, WRV, basis weight, incoming moisture, sheet temperature; load, felt type and life, felt cleaning, speed [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.4]
- "Nip pressure is usually the most important operating parameter. Keep it as high as quality limits allow." [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.4]
