- Freeness: how freely water drains from a fiber slurry; indirect indication of the amount of refining via fines from the P and S1 layers (and cutting); high freeness = high drainage rate. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.12]
- Smook Fig 13-23: tensile and burst rise as CSF falls from about 800 to 500; tear peaks near 800 CSF then falls; freeness feedback control uses a drainage sensor, see [[refiner-control-and-metering-blending]]. [DOC 01a335fe-bfba-4f11-b32c-df2de03d8521 p.5]
- CSF is the most common freeness test in North America. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.13]
- Sensitive to sample weight (surface area), stock temperature (fix or compensate), and fines content. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.14]
- Typical CSF: unrefined chemical pulps 700+; refined chemical 400-500; refined mechanical 100-300; groundwood for newsprint 40-100. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.15]
- Other tests: Schopper-Riegler (Europe), Williams Slowness Tester, Drainage Resistance Analyzer, TAPPI Drainage. [DOC 856ff3f6-9be4-42e7-b4dc-fee63dfd3cfd p.16]

## Smook handbook (see [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-12]], [[pulp-testing-and-lab-beating]])
- CSF is the volume of water (mL) from the side orifice when a 0.30% consistency suspension at 20 C drains through the screen plate; freeness and slowness are inversely related [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.21]
- Schopper-Riegler slowness tester is the main laboratory drainage instrument in Europe [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.21]
- Fines passing 200 mesh are the main cause of reduced drainage; removing fines can restore drainage with strength unchanged [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.21]
- On-line freeness: Drainac III style sensor times filtrate rise in a tube; automatic devices have had mixed success [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.22]
- Pulp strengths are traditionally compared at 500 CSF and 300 CSF by interpolating the beater curve [DOC 30d870b7-8b32-475a-9f65-e8f3bd87bd7b p.24]
## AFT manual ([[doc-aft-intro-stock-prep-refining]])
- CSF: 1 L at 0.3% consistency; up to 750 ml for unrefined unbleached softwood kraft, 30-40 ml for fine groundwood; stock prep refining of kraft typically 600 to 250 ml. [DOC 573e2f9c-afca-42a5-b4a6-10980694ab2e p.15]
- Freeness predicts sheet density; "most widely used control test in the stock preparation area" in North America; SR, Williams slowness, TAPPI drainage time roughly convertible to CSF. [DOC 573e2f9c-afca-42a5-b4a6-10980694ab2e p.15]
