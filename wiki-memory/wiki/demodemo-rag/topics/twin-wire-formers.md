## Why twin-wire
- Benefits: faster drainage, less two-sidedness, avoids surface instabilities; by the late 1970s twin-wire was the dominant type for new large machines. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.7]
- Tidcombe patent 1881 anticipated a commercial former introduced 1968. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.6]

## Roll formers
- Jet captured between two wires wrapping a rotating roll, no relative motion wire to roll; good retention of fines/filler, poor formation (grainy flocs). [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.8]
- Membrane equation p = T/R (Eq. 6); Bernoulli balance U_r = sqrt(U_j^2 - 2T/(rho R)) (Eq. 7), so jet speed must exceed roll speed, giving MD shear and MD fibre orientation. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.9]
- Typical consistency leaving a roll former's forming roll about 6%. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.11]

## Blade formers
- Vertiforma installed 1968 in Quebec: very good formation, poor retention, hard to control. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.9]
- Bel-Baie II: jet flows upward, blades on one side on a large-radius support; good formation, poor retention, some two-sidedness. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.10]
- Counter-blades (loadable, e.g. Bel Baie IV) move perpendicular to the wires to change wrap angle for operator control. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.11]

## Roll-blade and hybrid
- Roll-blade (Valmet, mid-1980s): consistency after roll dewatering about 1.5 to 2.0%, versus about 6% for a plain roll former. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.11]
- Hybrid = Fourdrinier table then twin-wire section; popular as retrofits for more drainage and less two-sidedness. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.12]

## Blade pressure-pulse theory
- Zhao and Kerekes (PAPTAC 1994, journal 1996) gave the first rigorous model; Zahrai and Bark (1995) 2-D model agreed with 1-D within a few percent. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.13]
- Pressure rises with wire speed and forms upstream of blade edges; wide blades give a pulse at each edge but only the leading edge gives two-sided drainage; pulses merge at about 10 mm blade width. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.14]
- Fig. 19: roughly 3300 N/m2 at 8 m/s to about 7500 N/m2 at 18 m/s (speeds 8-18 m/s). [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.14]
- Under some conditions Eq. 9-10 are complex, implying pressure oscillations upstream of the blade. [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.14]

## Formation, retention, orientation
- MD pressure gradients drive flow relative to the wire when shear exceeds the suspension yield stress; cumulative displacement over blades correlates approximately (linearly) with formation improvement (Fig. 21). [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.15]
- The same large pressures drive high drainage velocity through the sheet, hence poor retention; MD flow orients fibres in MD, raising MD/CD strength but lowering z-direction strength (Hasuike 1995). [DOC 8e89cddd-56fe-4ef8-9aca-84a8ffe2a0f6 p.16]
- Hubbe review: at a forming blade the two-fabric sandwich sees a quick pressure rise then abrupt fall, displacing mid-web material against machine direction by 3 to 9 mm (Zhao and Kerekes 1996), improving formation and MD alignment; excessive blade action lowered strength, especially internal bond. [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.10, p.33]
## Smook 16.6 view ([[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-09]] pp.18-20)
- First modern twin-wire former: D. Webster, 1953; four categories: gap formers, hybrids, multiply formers, tissue formers. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.18]
- Blade formers form better but retain less and need more drive power; roll formers retain better but give grainy formation, so pure roll formers are rarely used now. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.18]
- Roll/blade formers seen as the best path for future development. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.19]
- Hybrids are mostly retrofits of fourdriniers: Bel-Bond (vacuum, removes more than 50% of headbox flow, about 40% up / 60% down) and Bel-Roll (about 40%). [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.19]
- Fourdrinier top surface can reflocculate without strong shear; top formers add shear at the second-wire nip. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.20]
