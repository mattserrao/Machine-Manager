## Functions and design
- Six functions: evaporate residual moisture, burn organics, supply heat for steam, reduce oxidized sulfur to sulfide, recover inorganics as smelt, condition combustion products [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.6]
- Tomlinson prototype built with B&W in 1934; C-E entered 1936-1938; since about 1968 most new North American units have no direct-contact evaporator ("low-odor", larger economizer) [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.6]
- Table 10-2: B&W sprays 20-40 psig onto walls, air about 50% primary / 30% secondary / 20% tertiary; C-E sprays 12-20 psig into center, about 65% primary / 35% secondary, no tertiary [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.6]
- Furnace must run under draft (negative pressure) for worker safety at nozzles and smelt spouts [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.9]
- Wall construction: C-E tangent tubes seal-welded; B&W membrane wall with 1-inch filler bars; both 3-inch tubes [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.9]
- Final superheated steam temperature may reach 480 C [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.9]

## Air and operation
- Primary air 50-65% of requirement; secondary/tertiary at higher velocity; tangential jets in boilers up to 1984 found to promote a cone-shaped body of combustibles [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.6]
- Excess oxygen about 1-2% (roughly 5-10% excess air); reduction efficiency depends mainly on primary air ratio [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.12]
- Sticky ash point of sodium salts about 800 C, lowered by chloride and potassium; solids must be cooled below fusion temperature before the superheater [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.9]

## Fouling and capacity
- "after 2 to 6 months of operation a recovery boiler will generally plug on the fireside"; waterwash requires burning out the bed, shutdown, cooling, then hot water through sootblowers [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.10]
- Capacity is exceeded when superheater-zone ash is sticky and soot blowers cannot control fouling [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.10]
- Older units rated by solids burned per 24 h, some run at about 140% of rating; newer units rated by thermal loading with less cushion [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.12]

## Thermal efficiency
- Older design without liquor oxidation about 61% (Fig 10-24 heat available for steam 61.22% of 677,515 BTU per 100 lb solids); modern low-odor up to about 68% [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.12]
- Rules of thumb: +15 C exit gas costs about 1%; -2% liquor concentration costs about 1%; salt cake at 75 lb per ton pulp costs about 1%; minimum outlet about 130 C to avoid acid condensation [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.13]
- Soda makeup 20-30 lb Na2O per oven-dry ton pulp; soda recovery cycle efficiency 97-98% [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.13]

## Safety
- Smelt-water explosions (from tube leaks) have been a problem since the 1930s; emergency shutdown procedures; BLRBAC sets standards [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.14]
- Smelt dissolving: steam shatter jet plus recirculating spray, oversized vent on dissolving tank [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.14]
- Alternatives: black liquor gasification not fully demonstrated as of the text; kraft recovery cited as proven but high capital cost [DOC 0f514475-3eac-427f-bd93-d1e763e9fe34 p.26]
- Oxygen delignification raises recovery boiler solids load about 4% softwood, 3% hardwood: see [[oxygen-delignification]]
