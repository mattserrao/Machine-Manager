## Ash and doctor blades
- High ash hurts tissue machine productivity: reduced drainage, increased creping/doctor blade wear, more ash in white water (higher ash retention), reduced felt and wire life, more dust [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.50]
- "Low ash is required to protect the creping blade" [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.43]
- Creping is done by the Yankee's doctor blade scraping dry paper off the cylinder; Yankee is a single large steam-heated cylinder with hot air hood [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.35]
- "Doctor blades are 'consumables'. Wear rates can be high!" Wear changes contact geometry and can change performance during the run [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.37]
- Low ash means more washing and lower yield; the example process has two significant washing steps [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.45]
- Stickies on tissue machines: build up at doctor blades and rolls, holes and breaks, downtime to clean wires and felts [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.51]

## Tissue facts
- Brightness: premium 75-80 ISO minimum; industrial toilet tissue 60-70 ISO minimum [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.43]
- Recovered paper may be up to 50% cheaper than virgin; US mix about 30% away-from-home (low quality) and 70% at-home [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.39]
- Illustrative prices on the slide: recovered paper $100/ton, virgin pulp $600/ton, finished tissue $30,000/ton [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.40]
- Softness: recycled fibers are stiff; multilayer headboxes can hide recycled fibers between virgin layers; a little virgin in mostly recycled improves strength (long fibers) or softness (short fibers), needing separate stock prep lines [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.47]
- Appearance: ink removal under 100% so specks are present; lower brightness [DOC c93229dc-2711-42b6-9211-821d0e0902e1 p.53]
