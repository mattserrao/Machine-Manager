Source [[doc-smook-fundamentals-part-04]]. Cooking theory: [[kraft-pulping-and-cooking]]; sulfite digester: [[sulfite-pulping]].

- Continuous digesters came into service in the late 1950s/early 1960s; lower energy and pollution confirmed, higher yield/strength not clearly shown; one unit down stops the line [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.9]
- Table 8-1: continuous favours lower steam, compact, diffusion washing; batch favours reliability, grade flexibility, mixed softwood/hardwood, turpentine recovery [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.9]
- Modern batch vessels 7,000-9,400 ft3 (about 200-265 m3), up to 19 tons pulp per batch; carbon steel, stainless liner for prehydrolysis, brick for sulfite [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.9]
- Steam chip spreaders pack about 20% more chips; bottom steam injection gains 5-10% [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.10]
- Forced circulation preferred; pump turns liquid over about once every ten minutes; maximum temperature held until target H-factor, then blow valve opened [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.10]
- Page-level coverage only (no values): RDH/Super Batch p.12-13; M&D and Kamyr p.13-16; MCC, EMCC, ITC, Lo-Solids p.17-18; IMPCO sawdust p.19-20 [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.18]
- Blow heat: conventional batch blow releases about 2000 lb steam per ton pulp, flashing from about 100 psig; cook cools about 170 C to 105 C [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.21]
- Blow heat recovery (Rosenblad): flash steam condensed in direct-contact condenser, hot contaminated condensate heats clean wash water via exchanger; accumulator interface moves with blow cycle [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.22]
- Blow condensate and non-condensables are high-BOD, odorous (reduced sulphur); usually incinerated in lime kiln, underflow steam-stripped [DOC b439a355-b41d-42a4-9c04-727066254493 p.22]
