## Pocket ventilation
- Water vapour build-up in the pocket lowers the partial pressure difference that drives evaporation; adequate ventilation is needed [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.9]
- Before about 1960 wool felts were nearly impermeable; from the early 1960s synthetic open fabrics let air move through on their own; air displacement depends mainly on felt permeability and speed [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.13]
- Fig 17-18 curves for felt permeability 140, 280, 500, 1000 cfm/ft at 1/2 inch water gauge [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.13]
- Active ventilation: hot dry air through the felt roll or an external duct; variable supply across the machine can correct moisture profile [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.13]
- Vapour pressure in the pocket: without ventilation up to about 15 inHg mid-pocket; with ventilation flat at about 7 to 8 inHg [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.14]

## Flutter and dryer layouts
- Flutter is billowing/flapping in open draws, causing creases and breaks at the edges; worst on faster, lightweight machines [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.14]
- Fixes: fabrics with lower permeability at the edges, changed pocket geometry, air doctors [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.14]
- Serpentine felt run (older machines, first section): bottom felt removed, top felt wraps upper and lower dryers [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.14]
- Single-tier wet end on modern high-speed lightweight machines: suction rolls between cylinders (Beloit 600 mm), blow box above each [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.14]
- Dryer threading at full speed with a dual carrier rope system; modern machines use ropeless air-jet threading with vacuum chambers in bottom rolls [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.14, p.15]

## Hoods and heat recovery
- Hood air use is "7 to 20 pounds of air per pound of water evaporated, depending on hood type" [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.15]
- Exhaust air must be hot enough to prevent drips, buildup and corrosion [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.15]
- Hood types: open/uninsulated, insulated open, totally enclosed; modern high-dew point hoods are sealed and insulated with partial recirculation [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.15]
- Air-to-air heat exchanger recovers exhaust heat into supply air; recovery into supply air "typically limited to 10 to 15%" [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.15]
- Sankey (Fig 17-27): supplied heat 100%, about 20% lost, about 80% recoverable secondary heat; supply-air recovery cuts heat requirement 10-15% [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.16]
