## Reeling
- Drum reel is motor-driven under enough load (amperage) to ensure adequate tension from the calenders [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.22]
- Turn-up: new spool on primary arms accelerated to machine speed by a rubber wheel, loaded against the drum; backtender breaks the sheet at the billowing loop; full reel removed by crane [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.23]
- Pope reel designed in the 1950s; 1990s reel upgrades address air entrapment and control of tension and nip load; some designs replace primary arm carriages with a moving reel drum [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.23]

## Drives
- Sections must be independently speed-controlled; mismatched speeds pull the sheet apart or give a slack sheet that folds and breaks [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.23]
- Mechanical line-shaft drives common until the 1960s; then adjustable-voltage DC sectional drives with analog regulators [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.23]
- Digital regulator accuracy within 0.01% of top speed vs 0.1% for analog [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.23]
- AC variable-frequency drives developed early 1980s, drive of choice in the 1990s; AC motors are more reliable and cost-effective [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.23]

## Winding
- Edges of the reel (typically 3-5 cm) are trimmed and conveyed back to the dry-end pulper [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.24]
- Table 17-3 winder components: unwind stand, tensioning roll, slitting station, spreader bar, rewind unit [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.25]
- Two rewind rolls run at slightly different speeds to give wound-in tension [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.25]
- "The winder drive must be capable of speeds 2-1/2 to 3 times faster than the paper machine" [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.25]
- Newsprint roll finishing example: 2,000 rolls per day with a three-person crew; post-1990 pressrooms use 50-inch rolls [DOC 212d6dd4-0711-4fdc-8e33-735d3a13d5de p.26]
