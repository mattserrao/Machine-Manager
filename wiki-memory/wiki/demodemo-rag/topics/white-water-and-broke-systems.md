- White water = drainage from wet stock; "rich" about 0.01-0.02% consistency (cutoff arbitrary); richest at the jet impingement area. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.20]
- Short circulation: rich white water from forming reused to dilute thick stock to the headbox; long circulation: all other white water, where fibre recovery and cleaning happen. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.20]
- Objectives: adequate supply of quality water in normal and upset conditions; steady headbox fines; maximum reuse. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.21]
- Guidelines: return fines to the headbox by the shortest route; keep broke and fines recovery separate; enough storage for chest swings; sewer only excess clear white water; minimise saveall load swings; "Give each paper machine its own white water system." [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.21]
- Closure: most mills cut fresh water 80-90% over two decades; costs are dissolved solids buildup, drainage problems, more corrosion and deposits. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.21]
- Fibre recovery: flotation saveall (air bubbles carry fibre/filler to surface) and disc filter (sector discs, filtrate through hollow shaft); drum filters also used. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.21]
- Broke: wet broke from forming and press, dry broke from dryers, calenders, reel, winder; system must handle maximum tonnage during threading and breaks plus small continuous flows (couch trim, winder trim, slab-off). [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.22]
- Couch pit: agitators, low-volume showers normally; high-volume deluge showers on a break; high-volume pump at a set level sends slurry to storage. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.22]
- Dry-end broke pulper: heavier agitation and deflaking, longer retention; recirculation prevents "festooning" and plugging. [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.22]
