## Core finding
- "density (fiber bonding) is a major factor affecting compressive strength"; for well-bonded sheets fibre modulus and strength may limit the ultimate edgewise strength. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.4]
- Effects of density changes are "more than twice as great as changes in fiber moduli". [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.11]
- Edgewise compressive, burst and tensile strength rise roughly linearly with density; STFI and Weyerhaeuser lateral support tests show the same shape at different magnitudes. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.10]
- CD compressive strength dominates top-load compression; MD compressive is needed for flat crush rigidity. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.5]
- Compressive strength is much lower than tensile strength at practical densities, with much lower strain at failure. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.15]
## Failure mechanism
- Under load the sheet expands and voids enlarge; bond disruption along planes of weakness; Sachs and Kuster: voids, tearing of cell walls, parting of S1/S2. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.10]
- IPC speculates strength is limited by bending and buckling instability of weakly bonded layers; transverse shear modulus and in-plane moduli relate well to strength and could in principle be measured on-machine. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.20] [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.21]
## Fibre and yield
- Earlywood and latewood sheets have similar compressive strength at equal density (similar moduli) though tensile differs. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.17]
- Fellers: compressive strength about constant over yield 48-66% at constant density; tensile falls with yield; lignin helps in compression, not tension. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.18]
- Seth: strength rises with wet pressing then plateaus; text prints a slight maximum at "5.2%" yield (likely 52%, as printed). [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.11]
- NSSC hardwood and recycled corrugated have low tensile but good compressive potential at given density; medium fraction of old corrugated has high compressive strength. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.19] [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.20]
- Z-direction bond strength: compressive strength rises fast to a critical level, then slowly; high consistency forming raises internal bond and compressive strength but loses tensile. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.20]
## Densification by pressing and drying
- Wahlstrom: extended nips could raise impulse ten-fold; steam boxes to 70-80 C; dryness gains of 5-10%. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.22] [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.23]
- Andersson and Back: heating web to 90 C gave up to 8% more dryness; solids 38% in to 40-50% out at 90 C vs 39-42% at 30 C. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.23]
- Justus and Cronin (extended nip): burst, ring crush, tensile, density up 10-20% with higher dryness; drying energy down about 20-25% or more. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.26]
- Perrault survey of high-PLI double-felted presses: better pressing, usually less refining, no compressive data. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.26]
- Press drying (FPL) lifts compressive and tensile strength and Young's modulus but lowers tear; cold (room temperature) press-dried hardwood reached similar strength at equal density. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.26] [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.29]
- Ince: wood cost savings of $25-32 per metric ton projected from press drying high-yield hardwood. [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.30]
