## Operative values, doc pages 6-30 (wet end, white water, pressing)
- Perforated (rectifier) rolls: "Hole diameter: typically 2 to 4 cm. Open area: 35 to 50%. Rotational speed: 6 to 15 rpm." [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.6]
- Manifold recirculation is set "so pressures at points A and B match, indicated by no flow in the sight glass"; too little recirculation, pressure rises along the header; too much, it falls [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.6] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.8]
- Jet angle falls sharply as L/b rises; above L/b of about 2 the angle is close to zero (Fig 16-19) [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.10]
- Jet-to-wire speed ratio "usually kept close to 1"; jet slower = sheet "dragged", faster = "rushed"; jet speed inferred from headbox pressure [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.10]
- Dry line sits near the second dry box; a shift indicates a change in drainability, corrected by headbox consistency or dry box vacuum [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.8]
- With main and secondary bleeds both set properly the dry line is nearly straight [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.11]
- Dilution principle: white water added at many points across the headbox, now standard on new headboxes; keeps constant lip opening, avoids the fibre-orientation damage of slice screws [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.11]
- Plastic forming fabric lasts up to ten times longer than phosphor bronze wire (metal until late 1960s) [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.12]
- Machines up to about 400 m/min use a shake mechanism and rubber deckles; faster machines need neither [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.12]
- Foil angles "about 0.5 to 3.0 degrees"; foil assemblies hold 3 to 10 blades [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.13]
- CONFLICT inside the page: low-head vacuum foil text says "up to 20 inches of water vacuum", figure caption says "up to 10 inches water" [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.14]
- Flatbox Fig 16-37: water extraction about 12 lb water/lb fibre at box 1 falling to about 1.5-2 at box 7; vacuum about 2 in. Hg at box 1 rising to about 6-6.5 by boxes 5-7; boxes covers about 5 cm thick [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.14] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.15]
- Consistency limits on static elements: slow newsprint to about 2.0%; food board hard to exceed 1.5-1.7%; free-draining fine paper above 3% without vacuum [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.16]
- Design rule: "Drain as much as possible using static and low-vacuum elements before using high vacuum" (less wire drag and power, less marking) [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.16]
- Overspeeding the dandy up to 5% improved formation on some modern machines; modern dandy sits where consistency is about 2%, surface 35-40 mesh [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.17] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.18]
- Gap formers: first modern twin-wire former D. Webster 1953; blade formers form better but retain less and need more drive power; roll formers retain better but give grainy formation [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.18]
- Bel-Bond can remove more than 50% of headbox flow (about 40% up, 60% down); Bel-Roll handles about 40% [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.19]
- Rich white water about 0.01 to 0.02% consistency; short circulation vs long circulation definitions [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.20]
- White water system objectives (3), operating guidelines (6 incl. "Give each paper machine its own white water system"); fresh water use cut 80-90% over two decades [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.21]
- Web enters the press at about 20% solids [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.23]
- Dryness example: 40% to 41% dryness is 2.5% relative, but water-to-fibre ratio 1.50 to 1.44 (about 4.0%) [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.23]
- Four press-nip phases (Fig 16-60): compression/air escape, saturation and hydraulic pressure to mid-nip, nip opens to zero hydraulic pressure (max dryness), expansion/rewetting [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.23] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.24]
- "Lineal nip pressure = (mechanical load + top roll weight) / nip contact length"; actual pressure follows a logarithmic relation with nip impulse [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.25]
- Fig 16-61 at 45 kN/m: hard cover peak near 3300 kPa, soft cover near 2000 kPa; crown only about 1.3 mm (0.05 in) at mid-roll [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.26]
- Grooved roll (1963): groove 2.5 mm deep, 0.5 mm wide, 3.2 mm pitch; max lateral water travel 1.3 mm vs about 5 mm suction press and 20 mm plain press [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.27]
- Double-felting useful in the first position for flow-limited presses, and in 2nd/3rd positions for sheets above 130 g/m2 [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.28]
- Shoe press introduced 1981; first design 4100 kPa (600 psi) over 25 cm shoe = about 1050 kN/m; latest about 1500 kN/m (felt limit); pressure lasts up to about 8 times longer than a roll press; over 400 in service by 2001 [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.28] [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.29]
- Fig 16-75: extended nip plateau near 700-800 psi vs conventional spike near 2000 psi [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.29]
- Open draw remains on older light machines below about 600 m/min (2000 ft/min); suction pickup first used commercially 1954 [DOC 66857f06-84b5-470f-ada6-025ffe226134 p.30]
