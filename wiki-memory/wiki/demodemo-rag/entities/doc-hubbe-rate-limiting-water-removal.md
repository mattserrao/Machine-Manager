- Machines are often drier-limited; vacuum boxes can use one-fifth of machine electricity [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.2]

## Operative values (verbatim)
- "Many paper machines are drier-limited, meaning that the maximum rate of production is essentially limited by the ability of the system to evaporate water from the sheet" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.2]
- "vacuum boxes can consume one-fifth of the electricity used on a typical paper machine" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.2]
- Table 3 consistency ranges: jet 0.15 to 1.2%; hydrofoils 0.4 to 4; vacuum boxes 2 to 18; vacuum couch roll 14 to 20; wet-press 16 to 55 [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.5]
- "about 99.5% of the mass present in the jet coming from the headbox" is water; jet contraction factor "can be as low as 0.62" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.6]
- Hydrofoil diverging angle "often about 3 degrees"; blade widths about 50 to 89 mm [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.8-9]
- Vacuum flatbox slots "13 to 25 mm in width"; pulse "half of one ms to several ms" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.11]
- Vacuum couch roll solids "in the range of 15 to 23%" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.12]
- Crowding number N: 1 frequent contact, 16 connected, 60 rigid; headbox stock often 16 < N < 60 [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.31]
- No benefit from vacuum above "e.g. 40 kPa" (Attwood 1960) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.42]
- About 30% of water pressed from a sheet is taken up again (Jaavidaan 1988); felts re-fill "within about 3 ms" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.44-45]

## Operative values, doc pp.47-84 (verbatim)
- Process water temperature: "Process water in the wet end of a paper machine is often heated to about 30 to 50 C for this reason." [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.56]
- Steam box effect: "Francik and Busker (1986) reported an increase of about 1% dryness for each 6 C of temperature elevation in the wet press. However, the likelihood of sheet crushing also was found to increase with increasing temperature." [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.56]
- Entrained air: "addition of about 0.75% air, by volume, to papermaking furnish was sufficient to increase the dewatering time during handsheet formation by a factor of more than two" (Brecht and Kirchner 1959) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Air in headboxes "can be up to 4% on a volume basis, once the pressure is released at the headbox slice" (Lorz 1987) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Vacuum boxes: "a maximum of eight slots per box was recommended" (Miller 1998) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- Prolonged highest vacuum "waste vacuum pumping energy, they increase the amount of wear on the forming fabric, and they increase the electrical power needed to overcome the friction" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.52]
- Low incoming solids: web at "9.6%" solids pressed successfully for newsprint (Pikulik 1992); "only works for lightweight sheets" [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.54]
- Process-water fines washing: significant at 3% web solids, none at 9.2% (Eames and Moore 1976) [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.50]
- Main lesson: "it can be advantageous to employ a gradual, stepwise approach to dewatering, in which the pressures and forces are gradually increased." [DOC 7d704e0a-0ca7-4284-8fb9-dc6721c03b8a p.62]
