## Current picture
- Stationary syphons: dp 0.1-0.2 bar [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.15]; rotating syphons: about 0.5 bar at 500 m/min, about 1 bar at 1000 m/min [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.19]
- Flow-through steam: stationary about 10% [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.15]; rotating 15-25% [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.18]
- Flooded dryer: condensate rises only to half the dryer; symptoms are breaks at one spot, vibration, raised steam pressure [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.13] [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.19]
- Fear-hole above the syphon shoe restarts drainage after flooding [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.22]
- Scoops needed below waterring speed (about 500 m/min) [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.23-24]
- PdCV1/PdCV2 should be closed in normal production; keep auxiliary condenser cooling water low [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.4]
- Turbulator bars: +5-8% production [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.33]
- Steam about 20-25 Euro per ton (2013) [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.1]
- CONFLICT on regime speeds and waterring speed noted at the end of the operative values [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.31]

## Operative values (verbatim)
- Condensate rise: "at flooded drying groups the condensate may only rise up to the half of the dryer." [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.13]
- Stationary syphon manufacturer asks "differential pressure of at least 0,1 bar" (0,1 to 0,2 bar); with turbulator bases the shoe-to-shell distance is "4 - 6 mm". [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.15]
- Stationary syphon at ca 1000 m/min can drain efficiently "without differential pressure" if the syphon tube is correctly designed; about 10% of steam flows through as flow-through steam. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.15]
- Rotating syphon shoe is a cone "ca. 1 - 2,5 mm to the dryer"; "without flow-through steam - no drainage". [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.17]
- "At rotating syphons about 15% to 25% of the steam amount, which flows into the dryer, is used as flow-through steam"; example 1000 kg/h drying steam needs ca 1250 kg/h into the dryer; flow up to 40 m/s in the syphon tube. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.18]
- Differential pressure for rotating syphons: "at 500 m/min a differential pressure of ca. 0,5 bar is necessary, at 1000 m/min even ca. 1 bar"; reduce it when speed or basis weight drops; stationary syphons need basically no adjustment. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.19]
- Operator should watch drainage through the condensate-pipe sight glass "once a day". [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.19]
- Flooding symptoms: more paper breaks at the same spot in the drying group, dryer vibration, steam pressure raised at same basis weight and speed. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.19]
- Stationary syphon maintenance: check steam-joint wear first "after 5 years"; check syphon shoes visually "every two years" with the dryer inspection; teflon shoes preferred. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.20]
- Rotating syphon horizontal tube erodes behind the flange weld seam; holes let steam bypass the shoe, so "the condensate only flows out of the dryer, after it is half full". [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.21]
- Fear-hole: drill hole just above the syphon shoe lets steam whirl condensate out again after flooding, with moderate rise in differential pressure. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.22]
- Scoops: used below waterring speed (waterring forms "only at a speed of ca. 500 m/min"); board machine example ca 50 m/min; no differential pressure needed; steam trap limits flow-through steam; removing a scoop takes "4 - 6 hours per dryer". [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.23-24]
- Modern scoop syphon (bent tube) keeps condensate plug in the tube so only the steam present at immersion passes; stationary syphon at low speed would need 0,3 - 0,5 bar. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.25-26]
- Group example at 850 m/min: rotating syphons need "ca. 0,6 bar - 0,7 bar"; 3rd group could run 3,8 bar but 2nd group is held to 3,6 bar (dp = 0,7 bar), so drying capacity is unused. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.27-28]
- Two fixes: flow off to the auxiliary condenser (energy loss, cooling water) or a thermo compressor ("the drying group is bathing in its own juice"), which needs sufficient motive steam. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.28-29]
- Turbulator bars: 20-25 square bars, condensate layer ca 6-10 mm; flow regimes: pit to 350 m/min, friction-carried 350-500, ring above 500 m/min; ring thickness ca 6-8 mm adjusted by syphon distance. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.30-31]
- Turbulator summary: production increase "about 5% - 8%", more uniform moisture profile, "3-4 hours for the installation in one dryer"; not sensible on dryers 1-5 where a low heating curve (<100 C) is wanted. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.33]
- CONFLICT note: pp.31 gives speeds 350/500 m/min for regimes, while the p.31 diagram labels 0-300, 300-450, >450 m/min; p.18 says waterring speed ca 450 m/min. Berlin uses approximate values (ca 450-500).

## Operative values pp.1-12 (verbatim)
- "One ton of steam is about 20 - 25 Euro. When producing 8200 hours/year the steam requirement of about 100 tons/hour will cost ca.16.400.000 Euro to 20.500.000 Euro. Saving one ton of steam per hour leads to a reduction of 164.000 to 205.000 Euro per year." [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.1]
- Stationary syphons in the cascade example: "a differential pressure of 0,1 bar to 0,2 bar is necessary for an efficient drainage"; condensate vessel 2 is always 0,1-0,2 bar below the pressure behind control valve PCVA2. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.2]
- "Paper break: The condensation in the dryers drops immediately ... the steam consumption drops immediately after the paper break"; heating pressures should be reduced to a break-nominal value. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.3]
- "The amount of cooling water for the condensation of the steam should always be as low as possible. A big amount of cooling water means that also a big amount of steam is eliminated." [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.4]
- PdCV1 / PdCV2: PdCV1 opens 0 to 100% over controller output 0 to 50%; PdCV2 over 50 to 100%; "In case of normal production these control valves should always be closed." [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.4]
- "about 90% of all paper machines use overheated steam" (about 95% have the distribution layout that causes it); steam about 10 C over saturation at the main distributor. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.5] [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.6]
- Condensate moves with the dryer above 350 m/min; above 500 m/min centrifugal force forms the ring (about 6-10 mm, dryer diameter ca. 1450-1800 mm). [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.5]
- Pressure-reduction table (main steam 5 barg / 165 C): nominal 0,1 / 0,2 / 0,4 / 0,7 barg gives saturated 102 / 105 / 109 / 115 C but actual 147 / 147 / 148 / 149 C, overheating 45 / 42 / 39 / 34 C. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.9]
- Heat-up curve by individual control: first dryer lowest, next about 0,2-0,3 bar higher; light grades (newspaper) need 50 C to 65 C at the first dryer. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.8]
- Ventilation: air is pushed out by inflowing steam, "half an hour at a paper machine with 35 dryers"; cast-steel dryers should take "1 - 2 hours" to heat up again, so a vacuum plant is not needed for ventilation. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.11]
- Condensation example: 2 t/h steam at 0,1 bar overpressure (specific volume 3000 m3) becomes 3 t condensate of 3 m3; the volume collapse drives steam into the dryer. [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.12]
- Dryer speeds range from e.g. 40 m/min to 1300 m/min; ring forms at "> 500 m/min". [DOC f2d6b892-8f3a-49f6-bfb1-8f6beada12e7 p.12]
