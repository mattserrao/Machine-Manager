## Operative values (verbatim)
- "Up to 20% separately cooked hardwoods could be incorporated in the bottom sheet without any major effect on ring crush or tearing strength. These were accompanied by a loss of about 18% in Mullen." [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.32]
- Sproul: "For blends of up to 30% hardwood" CD ring crush was constant, burst down 19% (still 99 psi on nominal 42-lb sheet), tensile down 13 to 18%, density 610 to 530 kg/m3 [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.33]
- Sproul conclusion: "with judicious refining, blending and balancing of papermaking conditions, up to 30% of high yield NSSC pulp can be used on linerboard." [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.33]
- Shick and Snow: ring crush "relatively insensitive to changes in yield up to about 75%"; sulfite-sulfide-carbonate pulps at 70-75% yield could replace kraft at 50-60% yield for linerboard [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.34]
- Philippine hardwood 50/50 liners: starch size or raise basis weight 42 to 47 lb to reach burst of 100 or more; CD ring crush 80-85 lb unsized, 100+ with starch sizing vs 112 lb softwood control; commercial 42-lb liners averaged about 88 lb (Project 2694-13, 44 lots) and about 77 lb (Project 2697-3) [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.35]
- Top-load box compression of hardwood-mixture boxes "about 9% lower than the control" [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.36]
- Summing up: "hardwood additions of 20% or somewhat more could be used in linerboard if lower burst levels could be tolerated" [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.36]
- High-consistency formed medium (3-6%): about +20% CMT and compressive strength, +110% Scott bond, but burst and tensile about 33% lower [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.37]
- Table IV: compressive strength 15 vs 18 MPa (+20.0%), CMT30 230 vs 280 N (+21.7%), Scott bond 200 vs 420 J/m2 (+110.0%), MD tensile index 60 vs 33 (-45.0%) [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.38]
- Combined-board prediction: ECT predicted to about 7% from composite liner strengths, Pmy = k1(Pl1 + Pl2 + D Pc) + k2; plate-strength form averages about 5% accuracy [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.41]
- Exponent b = 0.75 ("same as used in the Institute box equation"); at constant basis weight Pl = K rho^0.5 W^0.5 (b = 0.75) [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.44]
- Brecht-Bachmayer: CMT = (K E t^2 W)^k, k = 0.65; with E proportional to rho^2 this reduces to CMT = (K W^3)^k, i.e. density drops out; the report finds it "did not apply well" [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.48]
- Conversions: "lb/inch x 0.17513 = kilo-Newtons/meter (kN/m)" [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.56]; "STFI = 1.436 x ring compression" [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.57]

## Operative values, doc pages 1-31
- Summary: compressive strength "appears to be relatively insensitive to pulping yield at constant density", so more high-yield hardwood and recycled fibre can cut cost; "For maximum hardwood utilization in linerboard, new technology is needed, particularly in pressing and drying." [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.4]
- "Box compressive strength is the single most important end-use attribute." [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.8]
- Combined board ECT depends mainly on CD edgewise compressive strength of components, hence density; flute buckling can occur in the short column test with lightweight components [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.7]
- Fig 4 (as read, OCR): at 700 kg/m3 STFI 27.3 Nm/g at fibre modulus 5.5 GPa vs 34.1 Nm/g at 7.6 GPa [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.13]
- Andersson and Back: heating to 90 C gives "up to 8% higher dryness"; solids 38% in, 40-50% out at 90 C vs 39-42% at 30 C [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.23]
- Wahlstrom: extended nips to raise press impulse ten-fold, steam boxes to 70-80 C, dryness improvements of 5-10% [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.22] [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.23]
- Justus and Cronin: burst, ring crush, tensile, density up 10-20% as dryness rises; drying energy reductions "about 20-25% or more" [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.26]
- Fellers: compressive strength relatively constant over 48-66% yield at constant density [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.18]
- Ince: wood cost savings $25-32 per metric ton from press drying high-yield hardwood [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.30]
- Cold press-dried IPC hardwood sheets reach compressive strength at least equal to FPL hot press-dried at equal density [DOC 168553e0-baa3-4e06-827d-54415678796a p.29]
